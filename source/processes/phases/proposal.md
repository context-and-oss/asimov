# Phase 1. Proposal

**Purpose:** sell a Scoping. Not the solution — the mandate to investigate the problem properly.

Three activities in this phase are delivery work rather than sales work, and belong to the delivery model:

| Approach | Described here |
|---|---|
| Sales / Presales — lead, offer, contract | No — see the sales process |
| Zone and precondition clarification | Yes |
| Change Readiness Assessment | Yes |
| Handover from sales to delivery | Yes |

**Zone and precondition clarification.** We work in Zone 2 by default, and customers below it are raised or clarified *early*. Early means in Proposal, not Scoping: if a customer will not give agents access to the codebase, that changes what we can offer, how fast we can deliver and what it costs. It is a precondition of the offer, not a result of the scoping. The Zone 2 one-pager customer agreement template belongs here.

**Change Readiness Assessment.** Assesses whether the customer can carry the change. Not agentic-specific, but it has grown more important: the faster we build, the more often the customer's capacity to absorb change becomes the constraint rather than our capacity to deliver.

**Handover from sales to delivery.** A defined handover: checklist, handover meeting, allocation to Scoping. We are the receiving party, so we describe how we receive. If the sales side describes how they sell and nobody describes how we take over, the gap sits exactly where most projects start crooked.

**Gate out of Proposal.** A signed agreement covering a Scoping (framework agreement plus project agreement) · the Scoping approach chosen · staffing allocated · zone and level recorded.

**Artefacts:** sales presentation, framework agreement, project/sub-agreement. No document code starts here — **A100 is Scoping's output**.

**Traditional vs agentic:** almost no difference. The only new element is the zone conversation.
