# Phase 4. Operations

**Purpose:** keep the solution running, and close the loop to the next engagement.

This phase has a distinct character: **it is the only phase where we are often not present**. Our most important contribution is therefore not the operation itself but the **handover**, and the quality bar for what counts as properly handed over.

| Approach | Described here |
|---|---|
| **Handover to operations** | Yes — it is the phase gate |
| Customer operates it themselves (Application Services) | No — but we define what they must receive |
| **Business Continuity** | Frame here, process detail elsewhere |
| Project release 2 | Not Operations — see the loop below |

**Handover to operations.** Required artefacts: **BC100** handover documentation (solution overview, recording of the handover, team overview), **E100** IT environment (access, contacts, system documentation), **E200** deployment procedure, and **D100**, closed here after living through the whole project. Plus project closure: closure report, evaluation, NPS. Nothing counts as handed over until the receiving side accepts it.

**Business Continuity.** Maintenance and continuation, run by us. Its working cadence is **Kanban**, described once under [Development](development.md) and referenced here.

**The loop back.** Operations is the most important source of the next engagement — the FDE's growth responsibility, the principle that every engagement is a pipeline source, and the older idea of a "project release 2" are all the same movement. The phase frame is drawn with a return arrow **Operations → Proposal**, and this page describes what to watch for to see the next opportunity.

**The second loop: back into the model itself.** The Project Owner initiates the evaluation, but the *owner of the delivery model* facilitates it, and the lessons feed back into the model. The model has an owner, and there is a defined path from a project's experience to a change in the model. Without it, this document slowly becomes wrong.

**Traditional vs agentic:** the level agreed in Proposal or Scoping applies here unchanged. L1–L3 are all workable in Operations. Being in production does not mean agents reach production — maintenance work in a repository is Zone 2. Agent access to production data or logs is a Zone 3 conversation, taken per customer.
