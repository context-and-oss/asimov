## The document chain

A100 · P100 · P200 · **P300** · **P400** · **P500** · D100 · D101 · E100 · E200 · BC100. Each is described as *what must be clarified*, with the two carriers (Office/SharePoint, git) and a note on how it is produced traditionally and agentically. D100's lifespan — opened early in Development, closed at handover to Operations — is stated explicitly.

The flow per feature: **D101 drafted → customer approval → build → test**, with D100 maintained alongside throughout and released at handover. Approval sits *between design and build*, not after it.

## Roles and the responsibility checklist

FDE · ASWE · Team Lead · Customer Owner · **Project Owner**. The responsibility checklist *(internal, not in this bundle)* is the operating layer: six areas, each line with a default owner and a per-engagement review that puts a named person on every line.

## Customer contact and cadence

A weekly update on time, budget and scope is mandatory; a weekly progress meeting; a monthly steering committee; expectation-matching throughout. Extended with the cadence requirement each Development approach places on the customer.

The practice itself is described in the disciplines [Cadence and meeting structure](disciplines/cadence-and-meeting-structure.md) and [Status reporting](disciplines/status-reporting.md).

## Level and zone

L1–L3 and Z1–Z3, agreed once in Proposal or Scoping. What the level means for how work is produced, what the zone means for what agents may access, and the separate Zone 3 conversation.

## Business Value Ladder

Not an approach in one phase — an instrument with three touch points:

- **Scoping:** the ladder is built in the workshop. What value is to be created, and what does reaching it require?
- **Development:** the ladder is the reference for prioritisation. Which rung does this feature serve? It gives a reasoned answer when a customer wants something in that serves no rung.
- **Operations:** the ladder is the scorecard. Did the value arrive?

**Responsibility for benefits realisation sits with the customer.** We own that the ladder exists, is agreed, and can be referred back to, and we can offer to help measure. The position: *we do not take responsibility for benefits realisation, but we make it measurable.*

The practice is described in the discipline [Value laddering](disciplines/value-laddering.md).

## The selection guide

Which approach to choose, per phase — and the combinations that carry risk. Approaches can now be mixed, so the guide must name what the bundles used to prevent:

- **Discovery + fixed price** — the classic trap: the price is locked, the scope is not. Either run an Analysis after Discovery before setting the price, or let the fixed price cover only a bounded MVP.
- **Analysis + use-case delivery** — the inverse: the customer pays to lock a scope, then runs a way of working built to change it.
- **Scoping approach vs estimation unit vs contract type** — hours bind to time-and-materials and fixed price; effort points bind to subscription; a Service Level Expectation is a different commitment again. All three must agree.

Neither combination is forbidden. The guide says so out loud, rather than letting a salesperson discover it in month four.

Written in full as [its own page](selection-guide.md), with what decides each choice per phase, seven risky combinations, and how contract type constrains the options.
