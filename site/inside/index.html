The three phases, and the frame

Design, specification, build. Each phase is one entry point, standing on its own method files and its own piece of your repository. One D101 becomes one plan, the plan its task specs, and those become code. The fourth column is the frame around them: project management, kept while the phases run.

  • Everything lives in your repository. Entry points write into it; personas and agents read from it. There is no copy inside the plugin to drift.
  • One rule keeps the layers clean. An entry point carries the conversation and no method. A method file reads its artifact and calls no skill. Two levels, never three.
  • Same name in every tool. What you type is /asimov-spec in Claude Code, $asimov-plugin:asimov-spec in Codex and @asimov-spec in OpenCode; the skill underneath is one file.
  • Personas and the advisor have no entry point. You ask in plain words: "review this as Athena", "what does the delivery model say?". They read and answer; they write nothing. What a skill is, family by family →
  • The frame is not a step. Project management is the engagement around the feature work: its own column, kept while the phases run, never a link in the chain. Its pages, a risk log first, land under project/, beside documentation/.
  • A plan is working state. The plan, its task specs and the ledger live under .asimov/ and are never committed. Each phase's commit message carries what was checked.
