How it works

From description to pull request, with four human decisions on the way.

Key Asimov writes into your repo Asimov reviews or builds A person decides The stop: no technology before the business signs

Lane by lane

  • You describe the feature.
  • Asimov interviews you, writes the business design, and stops. The technical section is declared open in the file. The personas read it as your signers.
  • Business approver signs the what and why. No technology chosen yet.
  • Asimov writes the technical half on a second run.
  • A reviewer, not the author, checks it is gap-free: buildable without asking a question.
  • Asimov cuts the design into a plan: one task per sitting, the names between tasks fixed once, checked before you see it.
  • You approve the plan at the cut with one word. Every task spec is then written and checked blind by a fresh agent, whose clean check is its approval; the plan goes ready by itself, and asks you again if it changed after your go. Coding does not start before that.
  • Agents build one task spec each inside /asimov-build, reading your conventions by path; per phase Baley reviews the diff against the task specs and Powell runs every done-when, and your go commits it with the ledger.
  • A person approves the pull request.

Where a decision says no, the flow goes back one box. People decide four times, and no command can move any of their decisions.

What lands in your repo

your-product/
├── documentation/features/   D101-*.html   every design, rendered, next to the code
├── documentation/specs/      <feature>/   the plan and its task specs, one folder per design
├── .claude/skills/           persona-*/    your personas, authored by interview
├── documentation/conventions/<stack>/      your house style, read by the agents
├── asimov.md                               the one file Asimov owns
└── .github/                                your pull-request rules carry the gates
