/d101-review

The review entry point for a D101: it selects the file, resolves the phase it stands in, and offers the reviews that phase calls for — the gap review against the bar (§8a business-complete while §6 is open, §8b gap-free once it isn't), plus the reader-in-the-loop persona reviews of the business design and the technical design. Runs the gap check itself and delegates each persona review to its skill; never modifies the D101, and never moves either axis — the verdicts belong to people.

Scope: The Design stage of the L3 pipeline (D100 §3, stage 1). System-level architecture lives in D100-Asimov-architecture.md; this D101 covers the /d101-review command itself. Its authoring-side counterpart is D101 — /d101-feature-design. The rationale for adding /d101-review alongside it is in additional-command-ideas.md §3.1.

§2Purpose & audience

Reviewing a D101 well means applying the bar its phase calls for and reading the document as the people it was written for — and nothing put those in one place, so authors ran the gap check and skipped the readers.

  • The reader reviews went unrun. The gap review shipped as a command while the persona layer (D101 — Personas) shipped as skills invoked by name, so the two reader-in-the-loop reviews were the ones authors never reached for.
  • The wrong bar gets applied by hand. Which of §8a and §8b applies is a property of the target document's phase, and a reviewer who does not resolve that first fails a business design for the §6 it deliberately left open.
  • Findings blur into verdicts. A review that ends in ready or not ready quietly stands in for the human reviewer (≠ author) gate on the specs PR (D100-Asimov-architecture.md §3) instead of making that gate cheaper to pass.
  • Persona coverage is invisible at the moment it matters. Without a roster in front of the author while they review, a team cannot tell whether the readers who should see this document exist at all.
Not for. The Spec / Code / Review+Test stages — those have their own commands and subagents. /d101-review reviews one D101 against the bar and its readers; it does not do cross-D101 consistency checking (terminology drift across siblings — additional-command-ideas.md §3.3, /trace), and it does not review code (that is Baley). Primary audience: developers in any product repo self-checking a D101 during Design. Secondary: its human reviewers, who read the findings and so care that the structure is predictable.

Non-functional — 5

NF1, NF4 and NF5 constrain the command's own behaviour and carry acceptance criteria. NF2 and NF3 do not, and each records why on its own row.

NF1
Read-only on the D101, write-only to its review notes. R13 is load-bearing — auto-fixes mix authoring with review and rob the author of the iteration step. Fixes happen via /d101-feature-design, not via the reviewer. Writing R44's review-notes sibling does not relax this: R46 draws the line at exactly one path, and it is not the D101.
NF2
Complementary to human review, not a substitute. The command catches mechanical and definitional gaps. The human reviewer (≠ author) on the specs PR (D100-Asimov-architecture.md §3) remains the substantive gate.
AcceptedEvery requirement produces an acceptance criterion (§6.2). NF2 places this command relative to the human gate rather than constraining it, so any criterion written for it would test the process, not the software. Context& lead (alb) · 2026-08-28
NF3
Complementary to Copilot's D101 instruction file. Process doc §3.1 says Copilot reviews first via .github/instructions/d101.instructions.md. /d101-review is the toolkit-local pre-Copilot self-check (see Q4); both reference d101-feature-design-definition.md so they agree on the bar.
AcceptedEvery requirement produces an acceptance criterion (§6.2). NF3 records a coexistence that lives in the team's process rather than in this command, which names Copilot nowhere and would behave identically if Copilot were switched off. Context& lead (alb) · 2026-08-28
NF4
No product-specific assumptions in the command body. Same as /d101-feature-design; the command works in any repo that follows the documentation/features/ convention.
NF5
A review must never punish a document for its phase. The single worst output this command can produce is a wall of Fail rows against a business design that deliberately hasn't written §6 — it would teach the author to skip the gate. R19 and R21–R24 exist for that one failure mode.
Out of scope — 7
OOS1
Auto-fixing the D101. Reason: separating review from authoring keeps findings auditable and gives the author a chance to push back on a finding instead of having it silently applied. Fixes happen via /d101-feature-design.
OOS2
Cross-D101 consistency checks. Reason: drift between sibling D101s (concept-name reuse, terminology) is a separate concern tracked in additional-command-ideas.md §3.3 (/trace). /d101-review reviews one D101 against the bar; not against its siblings.
OOS3
Implementation review. Reason: that's the S101 validator subagent's job (D100 §4.4). /d101-review operates at the Design stage only.
OOS4
PR-comment posting. Reason: GitHub-coupling is a fork point (D100 §8.4). The structured finding report goes to chat. The author transcribes findings into the PR description / commit message if they want them persisted.
OOS5
Deciding that a document has reached a bar, or moving either axis. Reason: §8a's verdict is the author's and §8b's the human reviewer's (d101-feature-design-definition.md §2); the phase chip is /d101-feature-design's to set and the status chip the business approver's. A read-only reporter that could also promote a document would be both author and reviewer of the same artifact (R25).
OOS6
Reimplementing the persona method inside the command. Reason: a persona review's method lives in its skill (persona-review-definition.md); duplicating it in the command body would drift from the skills and break the runtime-source-of-truth pattern (D100 §7.4). The command routes and invokes; the skill reads (R29).
OOS7
Recording which reviews were run in the D101 itself. Reason: a review-log is new document state on top of the two existing axes; the reviews are advisory and iterative. This is distinct from caching a review's findings to a disposable sibling file (R44) — that file is never the D101, carries no state a re-run couldn't reproduce, and is deleted the moment it's consumed. If a persistent record inside the D101 is ever wanted it belongs to the approval flow, not to a read-only reviewer.
4
Business design
Design principles, the concept model (the three reviews and the two persona buckets), the review flow with its error paths, the rules that gate which reviews apply, and the recorded decisions with their rejected alternatives.

§4.1Design principles

1
Findings, not verdicts. The command names what's weak, where, and why. It does not emit a "ready for review" verdict — §8a's is the author's call and §8b's the human reviewer's (≠ author), per d101-feature-design-definition.md §2.1 / §2.2 and §8b. Automating the verdict would let the LLM substitute for the reviewer-not-author gate.
2
Read-only. Fixes happen via /d101-feature-design, not in /d101-review. Separating review from authoring keeps findings auditable and gives the author a chance to push back on a finding instead of having it silently applied.
3
Same bar as the author saw. /d101-review reads d101-feature-design-definition.md and d101-feature-design-template.html at run-time — the same files /d101-feature-design used. The reviewer and author see the same bar; drift between author and reviewer expectations is impossible by construction.
4
Complementary, not redundant. Copilot's .github/instructions/d101.instructions.md covers the first mechanical pass on the specs PR. The human reviewer applies §8. /d101-review is a third check the author can run before either — focused on what Copilot is weakest at (counter-examples on conditional rules, named deciders on TBDs, anti-patterns from §7).
5
Orchestrate, don't reimplement. The gap review is the command's own work — it re-reads the bar. Each persona review is delegated to its persona skill, the source of truth for how that reader reads. The hub selects, routes, and invokes; it never copies a persona's method into itself.
6
The phase decides what applies. Which reviews the menu offers is a fact about the document's phase, not a preference of whoever is reviewing. A Business design document has no §6 to read for mechanism, so the technical-persona review reads §4 for feasibility of intent; a Full design document offers the full set. The status axis is read only — the reviewer shows it, never moves it (D101 hard rule 4).
7
Reviews stay distinct. Gap and persona reviews answer different questions and their verdicts belong to different people — the gap bar to the author (§8a) / human reviewer (§8b), a persona read to whoever the persona stands in for. The hub runs them together but never merges their outputs or blends them into one score.

§4.2Model / concepts

Three concepts the command adds on top of the D101 vocabulary it inherits from d101-feature-design-definition.md (D101, phase, status, the bars, gap-free): the three reviews it can run, the two kinds of finding they produce, and the two persona buckets it sorts the roster into. Everything else — how a persona reads, what the bars are — lives in the definitions the reviews read at run time.

The two persona buckets

The command discovers the whole persona roster (standard + custom, the way /persona-list does) and sorts each reader into one bucket by a single signal already mandated by persona-review-definition.md §7: a business persona is defined not to know the mechanism (that blindness is what lets it catch leaked jargon and code-altitude prose); a technical / feasibility persona is the one exception that does know the mechanism and reads §6. No new frontmatter field is needed — the persona's own declaration of what it knows is the bucket.

Business

A custom reader authored by /persona-new joins whichever bucket its Does not know field puts it in — the routing is by the same rule for standard and custom personas, so a team's own readers appear in the menu with no extra wiring.

§4.3Flow

Trigger. Two moments, not one: after a business design lands on disk (to check it against §8a and read it as its business readers before deciding §4 is settled), and after the technical design lands (to check §8b and read the mechanism for feasibility before opening the specs PR). The author runs /d101-review either time; the command resolves the phase from the document, offers the reviews that phase calls for, and runs the ones the author picks. Outcome: one report per selected review, in chat, cached once to a gitignored D101-<slug>.review.md sibling (R44). The target D101 file is unchanged.

§4.4Rules / constraints

Six conditional rules govern what the command offers, runs, and scores. Each is stated with the case where it fires and the adjacent case where it does not, per d101-feature-design-definition.md §6.3. BR1–BR2 govern the gap review; BR3–BR4 govern the menu and the persona routing; BR5–BR6 govern which findings are allowed to move a bar.

BR1 — bar selection

The phase picks the bar. Which checks run is a property of the target document, never of the invocation or of the author's request.

  • Fires when the resolved phase is Business design — check 6 is not run, and check 9 is (R19).
  • Stays quiet when the phase is Full design or unrecorded: check 6 runs and check 9 does not (R20). Asking for "the full review" on a business design does not change this — the bar is not a setting.
BR2 — Open is not a defect

Open, Fail, or nothing. A §6 that is deliberately unwritten gets its own severity, so a reader of the report cannot mistake it for a shortfall.

  • Fires when the open block is present, states the document is not gap-free, and lists real outstanding items — one row, marked Open (R22).
  • Stays quiet when the block is missing, an N/A stub, or a bare TBD: that is a genuine defect and the row is Fail citing check 9 (R23). Open rewards declaring the gap, not having one.
  • Never eight §6.x Fail rows against a business design. That output would teach the author to skip the phase gate entirely (NF5).
BR3 — menu applicability

The phase gates the offer. Which reviews the menu presents follows from the phase, so an author is never offered a review that has nothing to read yet.

  • Fires when the phase is Business design — the technical-persona review is offered against §4 only (feasibility of intent), because §6 is not yet written (R27), and it is the one review the menu does not mark recommended (R28).
  • Stays quiet when the phase is Full design — the same review reads §4 and §6 and joins the recommended set, which is then all three (R28).
  • The gap and business-persona reviews are offered and recommended at both phases; a review the menu does not recommend is still offered, and no unpicked review ever runs (R26).
BR4 — persona bucketing

Knows the mechanism → technical. A persona is sorted into the business or the technical bucket by what it declares it knows, not by a tag the author has to add.

  • Fires when a persona declares it knows the mechanism / reads §6 (e.g. Athena) — it joins the technical bucket and runs in the technical-persona review (R30).
  • Stays quiet when a persona is defined not to know the codebase (Poseidon, Hermes, most custom readers) — it stays in the business bucket. The rule is the same for standard and custom personas, so a team's own readers route themselves (persona-review-definition.md §7).
BR5 — findings that never move the bar

Template-conformance is reported, not scored. A finding about how the document is shaped is kept apart from a finding about what the bar requires, so tidiness is never mistaken for completeness.

  • Fires when the finding is §7's presence or phase-shape, a wall-of-text cell or bullet, or §2 framing bloat — it appears in the section walk and is absent from the bar table (R39).
  • Stays quiet when the same section also breaks a rule the bar tests — §4 written at code altitude is a bar finding and does reach the check table, even though it surfaced in the same row.
BR6 — §7 is judged by phase-shape, never by depth

Present and in the right shape is the whole test. The build recipe is authored after the gap-free bar, so how thorough it is remains the author's call rather than the reviewer's.

  • Fires when the phase is Business design — the pending stub is Open, a populated §7.1+ is a Flag naming a recipe written ahead of the contract it realises, and an absent §7 is a Fail (R38).
  • Stays quiet when the phase is Full design — build notes and an N/A — pure reuse stub both Pass, and only a leftover pending stub or a missing section Fails.
  • A thin §7 is never a Fail. Depth sits outside both bars (d101-feature-design-definition.md §4.8), so judging it would invent a third bar nobody agreed to.

Claude Code's tool allow-list grants a tool, not a path — the same category of guarantee the never-move-either-axis rule already relies on. §6.8Known constraints & gotchas

  • Bar drift. /d101-review is only as strict as d101-feature-design-definition.md — tightening the bar in the definition immediately tightens the review on the next invocation, and vice versa (confirmed in AC9).
  • No verdict by design. The command emits findings only; the "ready for human review" decision is the human reviewer's (≠ author) call per d101-feature-design-definition.md §2.2 / §8b, and emitting a verdict here would let an LLM substitute for the reviewer-not-author gate (process doc §2 rule 1).
  • Template-structure rigidity. D101s that diverge from d101-feature-design-template.html produce noisy reports; the command treats divergence as a structural Fail rather than reinterpreting (intentional — see Q6).
  • Candidate-picker ergonomics. No argument means "show me the list" — the developer always picks, so the wrong-file risk auto-detect carried is removed by construction, at the cost of one extra exchange when the path isn't passed inline.
  • Persona coverage is only as good as the roster. A repo with no feasibility persona gets a pointer to /persona-new, not a feasibility read; the three standard personas ship on install so the buckets are never empty in a plain Asimov repo, but custom coverage is the team's to author.
  • Bucketing trusts the persona's own words. Routing reads each persona's declared Does not know to bucket it (BR4), so a malformed persona that "knows the codebase" but is meant as a business reader routes to technical — a persona-authoring defect (persona-review-definition.md §7) surfaced by the routing, not caused by it.

§9Open questions

Numbering starts at Q4: this D101 and D101 — /d101-feature-design were split from one source document, and Q1–Q3 went with the authoring half. The numbers are per-document handles, so the sibling's Q4 and Q5 are different questions from these.

Open:

Q5Architecture (Context&)
Should the structured finding-report format align with the planned S101 validator subagent's output so reviewers see the same report shape across Design and Review+Test stages?
Resolution path: decide while spec'ing the validator subagent — the constraint flows from validator to /d101-review, not the other way.
Q6Architecture (Context&)
When the target D101 diverges from d101-feature-design-template.html structure, should /d101-review attempt section-mapping (best-effort) or fail-fast as currently specified?
Resolution path: the next real D101 will tell; the friction either justifies fail-fast or motivates a flag.
Q8Architecture (Context&)
Should the menu let the author pick an individual persona within a bucket (e.g. "just Hermes"), or only whole buckets (all business personas)? v1 offers the three reviews; per-persona selection is unspecified.
Resolution path: the first repo carrying several custom personas per bucket will show whether whole-bucket runs are too coarse; until then, whole-bucket keeps the menu to three choices.

Resolved during authoring:

Q4Resolved 2026-05-22
Does /d101-review run before Copilot's .github/instructions/d101.instructions.md first pass (faster local feedback) or after (catch what Copilot missed)?
Decision: Agnostic: the command does not reference Copilot in its prompt; the developer chooses when to run. Revisit if Copilot's role in the specs PR shifts.
Q9Resolved 2026-08-28
The built command listed candidates and waited for confirmation even when a path argument was supplied, which R5 did not require. Tighten R5 to the built behaviour, or honour an explicit path without the extra exchange?
Decision: R5 tightened — a supplied path is a suggestion that still gets confirmed, because a mistyped or stale path carries the same wrong-file risk the picker exists to remove (§4.5). Reopen if the extra exchange proves to cost more than the wrong-file risk it prevents.
Q7Resolved 2026-05-22
TBD — verify versus other valid TBD shapes — does /d101-review flag any TBD without — verify, or only TBDs without a decider?
Decision: Only TBDs without a named decider are flagged. The — verify shape is /d101-feature-design's output convention, not a d101-feature-design-definition.md requirement; enforcing it in /d101-review would drift the bar.
