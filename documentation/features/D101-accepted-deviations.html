Accepted deviations

A way to record, in the D101 and next to the thing it excuses, that a rule of d101-feature-design-definition.md is deliberately not met — naming the rule, the reason, the person who accepted it and the date. The review then reports it as Accepted on every run instead of asking the author to decide again. It never silences a finding, never covers a whole check, and is never written by the reviewer.

Scope: The Design stage of the L3 pipeline (D100 §3, stage 1). This D101 covers a convention that spans the definition, the template and both D101 commands — it adds no new command and no new subagent. §2Purpose & audience

A review that re-reports a finding the team has already considered and chosen to live with spends the author's decision again on every run, and there is nowhere to put the fact that the decision was taken.

  • The same finding costs a decision every time. One compound requirement was reported in three consecutive reviews of the same document; each round the author had to weigh it again, and nothing anywhere recorded that they already had.
  • A decision taken in conversation does not survive the conversation. The reasoning lives in a chat that ends, while the next review starts from the document alone and reaches the same conclusion the same way.
  • Silence is the only tool the author has. Faced with a finding they disagree with, the choice is to change the document or hear it again — so genuine accommodations get made by leaving the finding unaddressed, which is indistinguishable from neglect.
  • A reviewer cannot tell an accommodation from an oversight. An unmet rule reads identically whether somebody weighed it and chose it or nobody noticed, which is precisely the judgement the human reviewer is there to make.
  • Repetition trains people to stop reading. A report whose findings are partly known-and-settled teaches its reader to skim, and the finding that mattered goes past with the ones that didn't.
Audience. Primary: authors of D101s in any product repo, who decide what to fix and what to live with. Secondary: the human reviewers (≠ author) who own the gap-free verdict and must see every deviation they are signing off.
Not for. Weakening either bar. An accepted deviation excuses one named rule instance, never a check from d101-feature-design-definition.md §8 — a document is never accepted to the gap-free bar, only ever reaches it. It is also not a code-review mechanism: Baley judges code against repo conventions and is untouched by this.

Non-functional — 4
NF1
Visibility, never suppression. The mechanism removes a repeated decision, never a repeated report. The moment an acceptance makes a finding stop being printed, the reviewer who owns the §8b verdict is being kept from evidence.
NF2
Narrow by construction. The ceiling on what the mechanism can excuse is one named rule instance at a time — not a check, not a section, not a document. A bar that can be waived wholesale is not a bar.
NF3
No state outside the document. The D101 stays the whole record. Nothing to sync, nothing to migrate, and nothing that can silently disagree with the document it describes.
NF4
No product-specific assumptions. The convention names no product entity and works in any repo that follows the documentation/features/ convention.
Out of scope — 6
OOS1
Accepting a whole §8 check. Reason: it converts reaching the gap-free bar into being excused from it, and the bar is the only thing making the reviewer-not-author gate meaningful.
OOS2
A repo-wide or org-wide deviation policy. Reason: a standing waiver applies to documents nobody had read when it was written, and per-document records keep the decision next to the evidence that produced it. A deviation that recurs across D101s is a signal about the rule, handled by the maturity convention (Q2).
OOS3
Expiry dates and automatic re-review of old acceptances. Reason: it needs a scheduler and a definition of staleness the toolkit has no home for; the date is recorded so a human can judge age for themselves.
OOS4
Extending the mechanism to Baley or to code review. Reason: code has its own conventions layer and its own reviewer; this convention is about the D101 bar only.
OOS5
Counting or scoring accepted deviations. Reason: a number invites optimising the number, and the mechanism's whole defence is that each instance is read individually by a human.
OOS6
Recording who proposed a deviation. Reason: only accountability matters and that belongs to the accepter; a proposer field would invite accepting on someone else's suggestion without owning it.
4
Business design
The principles that keep the mechanism from becoming a waiver system, the vocabulary it adds, the life of a finding from report to acceptance to lapse, the rules that decide when a record counts, and the decisions with their rejected alternatives.

§4.1Design principles

1
Record, don't suppress. The mechanism removes the repeated decision, not the repeated report — a finding covered by an acceptance is printed on every run, with its reason and its accepter.
2
Next to the evidence. The record sits on the element it excuses, so it moves with the text and a reader meets it where the question arises rather than in a register they must think to open.
3
A name, or it did not happen. A deviation with nobody's name on it is an assertion that someone decided, with nobody answerable — which is the anonymous-TBD failure wearing different clothes.
4
One instance at a time. The ceiling on what a single record can excuse is one named rule instance, so the mechanism can never accumulate into a general exemption.
5
Accepted is never Pass. A deliberate state gets its own name, exactly as Open does for an unwritten technical design — so a reader mistakes it neither for a shortfall nor for a clean bill.
6
The reviewer never offers the waiver. Accepting belongs to the author and the accepter; a reviewer that proposes an acceptance is excusing itself from the finding it just raised.
7
A fix is the default and stays the better outcome. The mechanism is deliberately narrow and deliberately public so that accepting always costs slightly more than fixing, wherever fixing is possible at all.

§4.3Flow

Trigger. A review reports a finding the team looks at and decides to live with. Outcome: the decision is written into the D101 next to the element it excuses, and every later review reports it as Accepted instead of asking for the decision again. The finding leaves the author's queue, never the report.

Entry — the finding as a review raises it Fixed — the only state that ends the story Accepted — terminal, but not permanent Lapsed — the text moved out from under the decision Final node

Notation: UML state machine, per plugins/asimov-plugin/resources/diagrams/state-machine-template.svg. Accepted is drawn as a terminal state with a self-transition rather than as a final state, because the finding stops moving without the story being over — it resumes through Lapsed if the annotated text is rewritten. Only Fixed reaches the final node.

Steps
1
A review reports a finding — a rule of d101-feature-design-definition.md not met at a named place in the document.
2
The author decides: change the document, or live with it. Changing it ends the matter and nothing is recorded.
3
To live with it, the author records the deviation through /d101-feature-design, next to the element the rule was applied to (R2, R21).
4
The record names the rule, gives one sentence of reason, and carries the accepter's name and the date; the command asks for a name when the developer did not supply one (R4–R7, R22).
5
Every later /d101-review run reports the deviation, marks that element and that check Accepted, and judges everything else in the element normally (R12–R15).
6
The human reviewer reads the deviations alongside the findings and owns the gap-free verdict with both in view — which is what the record exists for.
7
If the annotated text is later rewritten, the acceptance lapses and the finding returns as an ordinary one (BR4).

§4.4Rules / constraints

Five conditional rules decide when a record counts and what it covers. Each names the case where it fires and the adjacent case where it does not, per d101-feature-design-definition.md §6.3. BR1–BR2 govern validity and scope; BR3–BR4 govern reach and lifetime; BR5 governs who may write one.

BR1 — validity

Four fields, or it is not an acceptance. A record counts only when it names the rule, gives a reason, names a person and carries a date.

  • Fires when all four are present — the element and its check read Accepted, and the author is not asked to decide again (R12–R14).
  • Stays quiet when the person or the date is missing: the underlying finding stands at its normal severity and the record is flagged as a suppression (R16). A partial record is worse than none, because it looks decided.
BR2 — scope

One rule instance, never a check. What a record may excuse is bounded by construction rather than by good intentions.

  • Fires when the record names one rule as applied at one place — "atomic requirement (§5), at R6" — and covers exactly that (R8).
  • Stays quiet when the record names a check from §8, or names a rule without a place: it is marked Fail as the anti-pattern a deviation that swallows a check (R18). Accepting a check would replace reaching the bar with being excused from it.
BR3 — containment

An acceptance never covers its neighbours. The rest of the annotated element is judged exactly as it would have been.

  • Fires when a requirement carries an accepted deviation for one rule — every other rule still applies to that same requirement, at full severity (R15).
  • Stays quiet when an author reads the record as covering the row: a requirement accepted as compound is still failed for having no acceptance criterion, because that is a different rule.
BR4 — lapse

The decision is bound to the text. An acceptance describes one piece of writing, so it cannot outlive it.

  • Fires when the annotated element is materially rewritten — the acceptance no longer applies, and the finding returns as an ordinary one needing a new decision.
  • Stays quiet when the edit is cosmetic and the reason still describes the text: a typo fix or a reworded clause does not reopen a settled question.
  • Nothing detects a lapse automatically. The reason sentence is what a reader compares against the text, which is why it names the specific shape being accepted rather than saying "as discussed".
BR5 — who may write one

The author records; the reviewer never offers. Writing an acceptance is an authoring act, and it happens where authoring happens.

  • Fires when the developer declines a rule during /d101-feature-design — the command records the deviation and asks who is accepting it if no name was given (R21, R22).
  • Stays quiet when the request reaches /d101-review: it declines, because a reviewer that writes or even suggests a waiver is excusing itself from the finding it just raised (R19).

§4.5Decisions and alternatives

A decision recorded without its rejected alternative is one that gets re-litigated.

Decided
Alternative considered
Why this won
Location: inline in the D101, on the element it excuses (R2, R3)
A per-document exceptions file; a repo-level review-exceptions register
A side file keys its entries to numbers, and numbers move — this repo watched one citation drift through three wrong requirement numbers across two renumberings. Inline, the record travels with the text and the reviewer meets it where the question arises.
Effect on the review: the finding is still reported, every run (R12, NF1)
Suppress the finding once accepted, the way a linter ignore-comment does
Suppression hides the deviation from the human reviewer who owns the §8b verdict, which moves a decision from the reviewer to the author. Removing the repeated decision was the actual problem; removing the repeated report was never asked for.
Severity: a fifth value, Accepted, that never resolves to Pass (R13, R14)
Report the element as Pass once a deviation covers it; or keep Flag and add a note
Pass would tell a reviewer the rule was met when it was not, and Flag would keep asking for the decision that was already taken. The toolkit already made this move once, inventing Open for a deliberately unwritten §6.
Scope ceiling: one named rule instance; a §8 check may never be accepted (R8, R9)
Allow a check-level deviation with a stronger accepter (e.g. the business approver)
A check is the bar. Making it waivable by anyone turns the gap-free verdict into a negotiation, and the seniority of the waiver only changes who conducts it.
Accountability: a named person plus a date, both required (R6, R7)
A role (“the team”, “architecture”); or no attribution at all, since the record is in a version-controlled file
Git says who typed it, not who decided it, and a role is nobody in particular when the deviation is questioned a year later. This is the same rule the definition already applies to open questions.
Lifetime: bound to the annotated text; no expiry dates (BR4, OOS3)
Expire acceptances after N months; require re-confirmation each release
An expiry needs a scheduler and a notion of staleness the toolkit has nowhere to keep, and it would fire on deviations that are still perfectly sound. Binding to the text expires exactly the ones that stopped describing reality.
Authorship: the reviewer may neither write nor propose an acceptance (R19, BR5)
Let /d101-review offer “accept this finding?” at the point it reports it
It is the shortest path to a document full of waivers: the tool that raised the finding would be offering the way out of it, at the moment the author is most inclined to take it.
Reach: D101s only — not code review, not S101 (OOS4)
One deviation mechanism spanning every Asimov artifact
Code already has a conventions layer with its own escape hatches, and S101 does not exist yet. A mechanism generalised before its second case is a guess about the second case.

§6.8Known constraints & gotchas

  • Nothing detects a lapse. BR4 is enforced by a human comparing the reason against the text, which is why the reason must name the specific shape being accepted rather than gesturing at a conversation.
  • The mechanism cannot tell a good acceptance from a lazy one. It guarantees only that a lazy one is signed, dated and printed on every run — the deterrent is visibility, not validation.
  • One reader today. /d101-review is the only tool that reads a D101 against the definition; any second reader added later must honour .accepted the same way, or a deviation this toolkit honours returns there as a plain finding (Q3).
  • Placement carries meaning. A record moved out of its element loses its instance and becomes a general claim, which BR2 then rejects — the nesting is data, not layout.
  • The two anti-patterns are what keep the mechanism honest. Without them an unsigned or check-level record would simply be believed, and the mechanism would become the waiver system it exists not to be.

§7.1What is genuinely new to build

Everything not listed here arrives by reuse. The section sits outside both bars (d101-feature-design-definition.md §4.8) — it gates no verdict and moves neither chip.

These notes are code-grounded design. Each was checked against the files as they stand in the plugin; where the built artifact already differs from §3, it is marked inline rather than smoothed over.
  • The definition section. §4.9 is the whole contract — four fields, three limits, and the rule that Accepted never becomes Pass; everything else references it (R10).
  • Two anti-patterns. Deviation without an accepter and a deviation that swallows a check are what make an invalid record fail rather than be believed (R16, R18).
  • A fifth severity. Accepted joins Pass / Open / Flag / Fail in the ladder the review already shares between the section walk and the bar table, so it lands in both tables without new machinery (R13, R14).
  • The markup shape. A .accepted block with three named spans, nesting inside whichever element it excuses — new, and the only rendering this feature adds (R11).
  • The authoring branch. /d101-feature-design gains a response to “we have already decided to live with that”, including asking who accepts it (R21, R22).
  • Reused, not built. The severity-ladder machinery, the section walk, the N/A-policy structure that already distinguishes N/A from OPEN from PENDING, and the anti-pattern list the two new entries join.
  • The worked example is real, not illustrative. NF2 and NF3 of D101 — /d101-review carried their deviation as prose no tool could read; they now carry it as two records, which is the mechanism's first live use.

§7.4Cross-cutting — shared by the four files

  • One definition, referenced by path. The template and both commands point at §4.9 rather than restating it, so the contract cannot drift between writer and reader (hard rule 3).
  • The mechanism mirrors one that already works. Accepted is built the way Open was — a deliberate state given its own name so a reader cannot read it as either a failure or a pass.
  • Nothing is added to either axis. A deviation moves neither the phase chip nor the status chip, and no command records one on the author's behalf.
  • The two anti-patterns are the load-bearing part. Without them the mechanism degrades into a waiver system on the first unsigned record.
  • Consuming repos pick the change up on plugin update, not on write. A D101 authored against the new template renders unstyled in a repo running an older plugin, though its four fields stay readable and reviewable.

§9Open questions

Open:

Q1Context& lead (alb)
Should an accepted deviation require an accepter who is not the author, mirroring the reviewer-not-author gate on the gap-free bar?
Resolution path: decide after the first handful of real deviations — if authors are routinely signing their own, the separation is worth its cost; if deviations are rare, it is ceremony.

Resolved:

Q2Resolved 2026-09-10
If one deviation recurs across many D101s — the same rule declined for the same reason every time — is that a signal to relax the rule in d101-feature-design-definition.md rather than to accept it repeatedly (OOS2)?
Decision: Yes, by the artifact-maturity convention — D101 — Artifact maturity §4.3 rule 3: below adopt, a rule declined by accepted deviation on three documents is reworded; at adopt the same signal opens a D101 instead.
Q3Resolved 2026-09-15
A second tool reading D101s against the definition would not know §4.9 and would report findings this toolkit honours as accepted. Should such a reader learn the convention, or is the duplicate report acceptable noise?
Decision: Deferred until a second reader exists — none does today (§6.8). When one is added it must honour .accepted the same way /d101-review does, so the record is read from the D101 and never re-litigated elsewhere.
