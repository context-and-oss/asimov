D101 · Feature design · Project frame

Issue log

The second artifact of the Project frame: one log per product repo for the questions the engagement waits on and the actions it must take outside the product's backlog, each with a named person and a date, closed only with what came of it, so the answered questions become the engagement's record of decisions.

Project (D100 §3)
Pieces
a path of asimov-project · 2 skills · 1 script · a shared core
Last updated

Lands in
project/
Scope: the issue log, second artifact of the Project frame (D100 §3): the log page, its path through the frame's entry point asimov-project, the two skills and the script that maintain and show it, the risk log's offer when a risk occurs, and the frame's pages on the Asimov website. The frame's shared rules live in D100 §3/§4.3 and CLAUDE.md, and the risk log's in D101-project-management-risk.html; this document names only what the issue log adds or changes. §2–§5 are the business agreement, §6 the technical design, §7 the build notes until the Spec stage plans it.

§2Why

The delivery model asks for an issue log reviewed every week, and the Project frame keeps only risks: an unanswered question and a follow-up nobody owns have no home.

  • Seen on our own projects: the answer that vanished. A question was answered aloud in a workshop, nobody wrote the answer down, and weeks later it was reopened by someone who had not been there.
  • Seen on our own projects: work stalled with nobody chasing. The team waited on an answer from the customer, and nobody was answerable for following up, so nobody did.
  • Seen on our own projects: an overrun nobody could explain. The delay came from clarifications that arrived late, but no record showed it, so it landed on the team.
  • Seen on our own projects: the meeting never called. A meeting to settle an open point was agreed, and nobody called it.
  • Seen on our own projects: closed as "resolved". A question was marked as settled, and what was decided is written nowhere.

Their cost on our projects has not been measured; the failures the design guards against are in §4.1.

Audience. The project lead who keeps the log, the assignees and owners named in it, the customer and steering readers who read the page the team shares with them, and the maintainers who add the next artifacts to the Project frame, the status report first, which will read from this log.
Success. One engagement's log has been maintained five times, by a write or a confirmed review, by someone other than its author; that is also the evidence that moves the issue log from assess to trial.

4
Business design
The principles the issue log holds to, its vocabulary, what a person does with it, the rules with their counter-examples, and the decisions with the alternatives they ruled out.

§4.1Principles

1
Engagement state, not solution documentation. The log is maintained and re-read for the life of the engagement, not approved once and then read.
2
The page is the log. Data, view and history live on one page in the repo; there is no second store to fall out of step with it.
3
Nothing derived is read back. Overdue is shown on the page, but it is always computed from the date, never taken from what the page shows.
4
The log follows a conversation, it never replaces one. Ask first, log second; Asimov keeps the entries and never writes the message or chases on its own.
5
Every entry is chaseable. It has a named person and a date, never a role and never "sometime".
6
Every close says what came of it. A question closes with its answer and who decided, a cancel with its reason, and "resolved" alone is never a close.
7
One fixed view, most urgent first. The log always reads the same way, with the overdue entries at the top and the counts above them.
The failures the principles guard against

The delivery model's issue-handling discipline names them; each is answered by a principle above or a rule in §4.4.

  • No needed-by date. Everything is open, nothing is urgent, and nothing gets chased (P5, IL1).
  • Closing without the answer. The entry is marked settled and what was decided is written nowhere (P6, IL7).
  • Never looking at the pattern. Forty open entries are one problem about how fast the customer can decide, and the counts above the list make that visible (P7, R46).
  • Logging instead of asking, and a log that is a case file. The tool cannot prevent either; P4 names them, and the people keep them in check.

§4.3What a person does

Where the issue log sits in the Project frame, and the eight things a person does with it.

The issue log in the Project frame

The issue log is the second artifact of the Project frame (D100 §3): its page lands at project/issue-log.html, a person reaches it through the frame's one entry point by naming it (asimov-project, "issue log, …", "question for …", "action: …"), and the frame's shared rules live in D100 §3/§4.3 and CLAUDE.md.

  • The log is engagement state; it hands nothing to a next stage and so has no stage contract.
  • The delivery model's advisor (pm-advisor) is offered in one line where the model has an answer, when a question is registered without a consequence and when a person opts out, and its advice is never written into the page unasked.
  • The status report, the next artifact of the frame, will read from this log (OOS8, Q2).
  • The delivery model's discipline knows only questions (its clarification log); the actions are this log's addition, recorded as a departure in the issue-log definition (OOS13).
What a person can do with the issue log
  • Keep it by asking. Register, change, close and hand over entries by naming the issue log to the Project frame's entry point, in Claude Code or Codex.
  • Read it as a page. The log is one page in project/ that the team reads in the repo and shares with the customer, with a filter for questions or actions.
  • See it in the chat. The same view every time, so the weekly review reads it top-down.
  • Find it on the Asimov website. The Project frame's pages say what the issue log does.
A week with the issue log

One engagement's week, the flows below in the order they happen.

  • Monday. After the workshop Maja Holm registers three questions: test data (Allan Borup, by 2026-11-20), the go-live window (nobody knows who decides yet, so Maja is both owner and assignee), and the archive rules (Allan Borup, chased again on 2026-11-18 since there is no real deadline).
  • Tuesday. She adds an action for herself: call the meeting that settles the go-live window, due 2026-11-14, referencing that question.
  • Wednesday. Allan answers "production data, but legal must confirm the anonymising"; the answer so far goes in the outcome, the question stays Open, and its date moves to 2026-11-24.
  • Thursday. The meeting is held; the action is Done with "the steering group decides, Erik Lund", and the go-live question is reassigned to Erik Lund.
  • Friday. The weekly review shows three open entries, none overdue; Maja confirms it with Erik Lund taking part, and hands her entries to Jonas Berg for the week she is away, role by role.
F1 · Register an entry

"We need the customer to confirm the test data by Friday." — the type is read from the words or asked when unclear; the type's required fields are asked for and the optional ones offered.

  • Outcome: a new entry, Open, with the next identifier, the history line Created, created and changed set to today, and the log updated today.
  • When no log exists yet, this first entry creates it, and the project language is asked for once (IL18).
  • When an opt-out stands, the log replaces it and its first history line names the opt-out it replaced (R43).
  • Told in another language than the project's, the entry is shown as it will be stored and written only once the person confirms or corrects it (IL18).
  • A required field still missing, or an assignee or owner given as a role: nothing is written, and the person is told what is missing (IL1).
  • Nobody knows yet who will answer: the owner is also the assignee, and finding the right person is part of the owner's chase (IL1).
  • No real deadline: the date given is the day the question will next be chased, so overdue still means "chase it now".
  • A reference to an entry that does not exist, or to a risk that is not in the risk log: nothing is written, and the person is told which identifier is unknown (IL15).
F2 · Change an entry

"Push the date to the 20th." — the person names an identifier, sees the current values and says what changes.

  • Outcome: the fields changed, one history line naming each field with its old and new value, and the entry's changed date moved to today.
  • Any field but the type can be changed in any state, and the state stays as it is (R19).
  • Changing the type: refused, and the person is offered to cancel the entry and register a new one that references it (IL3).
  • An identifier that does not exist: nothing is written, and the log is shown so the person can find the right one.
F3 · Close or reopen an entry

"The customer answered: production data, anonymised.", "Meeting held." or "Not needed any more." — the person sets Done, Cancelled, or back to Open.

  • Done on a question needs the outcome and the person who decided; without them the entry stays Open, and the person is told what is missing (IL7).
  • A partial answer ("probably, but legal must confirm"): written into the outcome as the answer so far, the question stays Open, and its date moves to when the rest is expected (IL7).
  • Done on an action with no outcome: the person is asked once, "What came of it?", and if they decline the entry is Done and shows no outcome written (IL8).
  • Cancelled needs a reason, and so does reopening, which keeps the identifier (IL9).
  • Outcome: the new state, a history line with the outcome or reason and the person, and the entry's changed date moved to today.
F4 · Review the log

"Where do we stand?" — the default when the entry point names the log with nothing else; the view is shown, the counts first and then every Open entry in full (R36), and the person is asked whether to mark the log as reviewed today.

  • Yes: one optional question follows, "Who took part?", which the person may skip; the log's last-updated date moves to today and its history gains a Reviewed line with the person and any participants; no entry changes.
  • No: nothing is written.
  • No log and no opt-out: the person is told so and offered F1 or F5.
F5 · Opt out

"We keep questions in the customer's tracker." — the person gives the reason, the place when it is handled elsewhere, and who decided.

  • Outcome: the log's place holds the opt-out, with its reason, decider and date, on a page titled No issue log kept here whose first paragraph is the reason.
  • An opt-out with a reason missing, a decider missing, or "handled elsewhere" without the place: nothing is written.
F6 · Bring the log back into working order

"Someone edited it by hand." — any flow that finds the log unreadable stops before writing and offers a repair instead, as the risk log does.

  • Outcome: a repair proposal listing each change as before and after; once confirmed, the log is readable again, nothing it held is lost, and its history gains a Repaired line.
  • The repair declined: nothing is written, and the log stays unreadable until it is repaired, by the tool or by hand.
F7 · Hand entries over

"Jonas rolls off on Friday; Maja takes over." — the person names who leaves and who receives; every Open entry where the leaving person is the assignee or the owner is listed with the role, and the person goes through the list.

  • For each role on each entry the person chooses: move it to the receiver, keep it, or move it to another named person; a question where the leaving person is both assignee and owner gets one choice per role.
  • Outcome: one write after the whole list has been gone through; each changed entry gains a history line naming the handover, and its changed date moves to today.
  • Done and Cancelled entries keep the people they had when they were closed.
  • A receiver given as a role, or a person with no Open entries: nothing is written, and the person is told why.
  • The person stops before the end of the list: nothing is written, not even the choices already made.
F8 · From an occurred risk

In the risk log's own flow, a risk is set to Occurred (RL17); the person is then offered "Register an issue entry for it?"

  • Yes: F1 runs with the type asked and a reference to the risk filled in, and the risk's where it is handled becomes the new entry's identifier.
  • When no issue log exists yet, the offer creates it, as any first entry does (F1).
  • No: the risk is set Occurred with the place as free text, as before.
  • The two writes are confirmed one at a time; when F1 is stopped, the risk is still set Occurred, with the place as free text.
  • An issue log that is opted out: nothing is offered (IL20).

§4.4Rules

What governs every entry and every change, each with the adjacent case it does not cover. A rule that ends (as the risk log's RLn) works as that rule of the risk log, with entries in place of risks.

IL1 · An entry needs its type's required fields

A question is written only with a name, an assignee, an owner and a needed-by date; an action only with a name, an assignee and a due date; every person is a named person.

  • Question "Which test data may the team use?", assignee Allan Borup, needed-by 2026-11-20: written, with the registrant as owner.
  • The same question with "the customer's architect" as assignee, or with no needed-by date: not written; the person is asked for a name or a date.
  • Nobody knows yet who will answer: the owner is named as the assignee too, and is reassigned when the name is known; no placeholder is written.
IL2 · The owner defaults to the registrant, on questions only

A question registered without an owner gets the person who registers it as owner.

  • Simon Vilandt registers a question and names no owner: the owner is Simon Vilandt.
  • An action has no owner at all; its assignee answers for it.
IL3 · The type never changes

An entry keeps the type it was registered with.

  • "Make ISSUE-004 an action": refused; the person is offered to cancel ISSUE-004 with a reason and register a new action that references it.
  • Changing ISSUE-004's name or date: allowed in any state (R19).
IL4 · Identifiers continue one series across both types

A new entry takes the highest identifier in the log plus one, counting both types and closed entries.

  • ISSUE-006 is an action and the highest: the next question is ISSUE-007.
  • ISSUE-007 cancelled and the highest: the next is ISSUE-008, never ISSUE-007 again.
IL5 · Duplicates are renumbered by the tool

When two entries share an identifier, the next write first gives the later-created one the highest identifier plus one, with a Renumbered from line, and only then makes its own change (as the risk log's RL4).

  • Two branches each added ISSUE-007 and both were merged, ISSUE-008 exists: the later one becomes ISSUE-009.
  • No identifier is shared: nothing is renumbered, and a gap in the series is never closed.
IL6 · Overdue is derived

An entry is overdue when it is Open and its date is before the day the log is looked at, on the page the day it is opened and in the chat the day it is shown.

  • Due 2026-11-06, Open, page opened 2026-11-09 with no write since 2026-11-05: overdue.
  • Due on the day it is looked at: not overdue; Done with a passed date: not overdue; a change that says "mark it overdue": declined.
IL7 · A question is Done only with an outcome and a decider

A question can be set Done only with an outcome that says what was decided and the named person who decided it.

  • "Production data, anonymised before it leaves the customer. Allan Borup": Done, and the history line names both.
  • Outcome "resolved" or "answered", or no decider: not set; the person is asked what was decided and by whom.
  • An answer so far, written while the question stays Open: kept in the outcome, and the question is still Open and still counted as open.
IL8 · An action's outcome is asked for once

An action set Done without an outcome prompts one question, and the person may decline it.

  • "Meeting held", no outcome: "What came of it?"; "nothing to add": Done, and the entry shows no outcome written.
  • "Meeting held; the customer chose option B": Done with that outcome, and nothing is asked.
IL9 · Cancelled and reopening each need a reason

An entry can be set Cancelled, or set back to Open from Done or Cancelled, only with a reason.

  • "Not needed: the scope changed in the steering meeting": Cancelled, with the reason in the history.
  • Open to Open is no change of state: nothing is written.
IL10 · An entry's dates

An entry's created date is set once; its changed date moves on anything that touches that entry, its fields, its state or its identifier (as the risk log's RL8).

  • A new assignee is set: changed moves to today, created stays.
  • A confirmed review: no entry's changed date moves.
IL11 · The log's date

The log's last-updated date moves on every write and on every confirmed review, and each move adds a line, with the person, to the log's history (as the risk log's RL9).

  • "Yes, mark it reviewed", with Erik Lund taking part: last updated is today, and the log's history gains Reviewed by Maja Holm with Erik Lund.
  • The log shown and the question answered no: nothing moves.
IL12 · History only grows

Every change adds a dated line naming the person, and no action removes or rewrites a line (as the risk log's RL10).

  • Closing a question adds a line; the lines before it stand.
  • A typo in an entry's name is corrected: a new line records the correction, and the earlier lines still show the old name.
IL13 · An unreadable log is repaired, never overwritten

A log the tool cannot read in full is not written to; the tool proposes a repair as before and after and writes it only when the person confirms, losing nothing the log held (as the risk log's RL11).

  • An entry lost its type in a hand edit: the proposal shows ISSUE-005 type: (missing) → asked and waits for the value and the go.
  • A log that reads in full: no repair is proposed.
IL14 · How the log is ordered

Open entries come before closed ones; the Open entries go by earliest date, then by identifier, so the overdue entries come first without a rule of their own.

  • An action due 2026-11-06 and overdue comes before a question needed by 2026-11-10.
  • The type never decides the order, and two entries with the same date go by identifier.
IL15 · A reference must resolve

A reference to an entry must name one in the log, and a reference to a risk must name one in the risk log when the risk log exists.

  • ISSUE-003 exists, or RISK-004 is in the risk log: written.
  • ISSUE-099 does not exist: not written; with no risk log page in the repo, RISK-004 is kept as written, since there is nothing to check it against.
IL16 · A handover goes role by role and is written once

Every role the leaving person holds on an Open entry is decided on its own, and nothing is written until every role on the list is decided.

  • Jonas Berg is assignee on three Open entries and owner on two: five choices, then one write with a history line on each changed entry.
  • Done and Cancelled entries are not listed and keep their people; the person stops at the third choice: nothing is written.
IL17 · An opt-out needs its reason and decider

An opt-out is written only with one of the two reasons, the person who decided and, for handled elsewhere, the place (as the risk log's RL15).

  • "Handled in the customer's tracker; Allan Borup": written.
  • "Handled elsewhere" with no place: not written.
IL18 · Content is kept in the project's language

When a person writes in another language, the tool shows the text it will store and writes it only once the person has confirmed or corrected it; the language is asked for when the log or an opt-out is first written (as the risk log's RL16).

  • An English project, "kunden skal bekræfte testdata inden fredag": shown as "The customer must confirm the test data by Friday. Correct?" and written only after the answer.
  • Written in the project's language: no translation step; names of people and systems are never translated.
IL19 · The type filter is a way of looking, never part of the log

The page always opens showing both types, a click moves between three views as the table in §5 S1 states, and nothing the filter does is stored or read back.

  • Only questions shown, a click on Actions: only actions are shown, and a second click on Actions shows both again.
  • A view chosen before a reload is not remembered, and two readers of the shared page always start from the same view.
IL20 · An occurred risk offers an issue entry

When a risk is set to Occurred, an issue entry referencing it is offered, and the issue log is created by that entry when it does not exist yet.

  • RISK-004 set Occurred in a repo with no issue log: the offer is made, and the yes creates the log with ISSUE-001, which becomes where RISK-004 is handled.
  • The issue log is opted out: nothing is offered, since the offer would replace a signed decision, and the place is free text.

§6.5APIs & contracts

One script, issue-log.ps1 -Path project/issue-log.html plus one action: -Read (data model, problems and the people named in the log as JSON; with -Person <name> also every Open role that person holds, the list a handover goes through, the name matched as the core compares names, and the names in use returned when none matches), -View (the fixed 80-column text), -Apply -Change <file> (write, check, rename), -Repair (a proposal as JSON, from problems or conflict markers) and -Finish (an interrupted write). §6.8Known constraints & gotchas

Encoding. The core is pure ASCII like the two scripts, because Windows PowerShell 5.1 reads a BOM-less file as ANSI; the page is written as UTF-8 explicitly.
The core is imported with -Force. The Pester suites run the scripts in their own process, and a module already loaded would otherwise hide a changed core.
The core reads nothing from its caller's scope. Every value it needs (the page, the prefix, the person, today) is a parameter; a script variable reached by scope works in one script and breaks in the other.
The core never calls exit. The risk log's Exit-Refused and Exit-Unreadable stay in the scripts; inside a module an exit no longer ends the script the way the exit codes of §6.5 and the unchanged suite of NF7 require.
The page's today is built from local parts. The overdue script forms the date from the browser's local year, month and day; toISOString() gives the UTC date and would mark entries overdue a day early for a reader west of UTC in the evening.
Two "todays". A reader east of the person who wrote the page can see an entry overdue that the chat showed as due today; both are right for their reader.
The filter hides, it never removes. Entries of the other type get the hidden attribute, so a printed or saved page with scripts off shows everything.
Line endings. As for the risk log, the check compares parsed data, never bytes, except for the two page scripts, which are compared with line endings normalised.

§9Open questions

Q1Simon Vilandt
When project setup exists, the log reads the project's language from it rather than asking: what happens to a log that already holds its own?
Resolution path: settled in the design of project setup, with the risk log's same question (OOS10).
Q2Simon Vilandt
What does the status report read from the log: the counts, the overdue entries, how long the oldest question has been open?
Resolution path: settled in the status report's own D101; the log keeps every date, so nothing it may need is lost (OOS8).
Q3Simon Vilandt
Should a question that goes overdue offer to register a risk, since the discipline says the log feeds the risk log when an unanswered question threatens the schedule?
Resolution path: decided after the first engagement's use; until then the person registers the risk and references it.
Q4Simon Vilandt
Should a reference also point at a design or a ticket, since the discipline says an answer feeds the specification when it changes what is built?
Resolution path: decided after the first engagement's use; until then a reference names an entry or a risk only (R20).

What does the issue log cost to build, and does that cost hold against the cut order in §4.5?
Resolution path: estimated from the plan's tasks once asimov-spec has cut this design; the cut order in §4.5 says what goes first if the estimate is too high.
