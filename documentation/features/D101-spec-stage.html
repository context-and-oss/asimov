Spec stage

One design, a D101 at Full design or an approved ticket, becomes one implementation plan (S101) and one task spec per task (S102), written by one asimov-skill with a built-in validation loop, the same file in Claude Code and Codex, so the author approves in chat a plan that fresh builders can build from without asking, and every task spec is approved by the agent that checked it blind.

Scope: the Spec workflow: the asimov-skill asimov-spec (with the re-check of a ready plan as a path of it, since 2026-10-07), four artifact skills and the S101/S102 templates, one file each for Claude Code and Codex, from any design that meets contracts/design.md: a D101 at Full design, or a Jira ticket. The Build workflow (dispatch, ledger, verification, Baley against the spec) is out. §2–§5 are the business agreement (§5 is the chat of one run, the feature's only surface), §6 the technical design; §7 is N/A.

§2Why the Spec stage

A D101 at Full design reaches a builder through chat today: the task cut, the order and the interfaces are decided invisibly, per run.

  • The graph lives in chat. Order and parallelism are re-decided every run.
  • One long task. The builder gets the whole feature: L2 with a file attached.
  • Names by guess. Two builders invent two names for the same thing.
  • §7 as a stopgap. Build notes live in the D101, where they do not belong.
  • Nothing to review between design and code. The reviewer sees the diff, never what the agent was told.
  • A bug fix has no design document. An approved ticket is its whole design, and it reaches a builder the same way: through chat, with no plan a reviewer can read.
Audience. The developer planning a feature, who approves the plan at the cut, the person directing a run, and the build subagents that read an S102 as their brief. In one line: asimov-spec asks at most five questions, decides the rest, shows the cut and waits for the author's go, which approves the plan, writes the task specs with their validators' approval and stops once; §3 is the contract behind that line, §5 is what it looks like.

§3Requirements

Functional — 30
R1
From one design, a D101 at Full design or any other design that meets the design contract (contracts/design.md, pointed at by the S101 definition §3.1), asimov-spec writes one S101 and its S102s to documentation/specs/<slug>/; the slug is the D101's, or the ticket key in kebab-case.
R2
A D101 not at Full design, or any other design that misses a required member of the design contract (contracts/design.md), is refused, with what is missing stated; the fix goes into the D101 or the ticket, never into a copy.
R3
Tasks are cut as vertical slices, one per buildable acceptance criterion of the design; two criteria share a slice only when the author says so (R21). Each slice is one phase in the S101; a criterion verified by review or by hand maps to no task.
R4
A slice that crosses stacks becomes one S102 per stack in the same phase.
R5
Every S101 opens with a Foundation phase: one skeleton S102 per stack.
R6
The skeleton is cut per slice: no two slice tasks that may run in parallel modify the same skeleton file.
R7
Every slice phase carries a tester S102 that writes the slice's acceptance test; that test is the phase's checkpoint.
R8
Every S102 is written for exactly one builder role.
R9
Every S102 is validated blind right after it is written.
R10
An S102 that fails validation is rewritten and validated again until it clears the buildable blind bar.
R11
An S102 that fails validation three times, or whose finding names a gap in the design rather than in the S102, stops the run; asimov-spec reports instead of rewriting.
R12
Once every S102 exists, the plan is validated as a whole against the dispatch-ready bar.
R13
A plan-level finding sends the affected S102s back into their own loop.
R14
asimov-spec named on a ready plan re-checks it: intact, it offers the blind re-check; edited by hand, it re-validates every task spec and the plan and asks for the approval again.
R15
A plan is approved by a person, a task spec by an agent. asimov-spec records the author's go at the cut as the S101's approval (who, date, a fingerprint of the plan's content), marks an S102 validated when its blind check clears, and sets ready only when the approval still matches the S101 and every S102 is validated. No skill approves a plan on its own; no person is asked to approve a task spec; no status is set by hand.
R16
Run against a design that already has an S101 and whose requirements and acceptance criteria are unchanged in content, asimov-spec updates the plan in place and leaves every S102 that already clears its bar untouched.
R17
Run against a design whose requirements or acceptance criteria have changed in content since the S101 was written, asimov-spec rewrites the plan: the S101 and every S102 are written anew and S102 files no longer in the graph are deleted. Git carries the previous plan.
R18
Validation warns, never refuses, when an S102 exceeds the size thresholds; the thresholds live in the S101 definition.
R19
asimov-spec explores the design and the repo before it asks anything.
R20
asimov-spec asks at most 5 questions, one at a time, each with a recommended default; the go at the cut (R24) is not one of them.
R21
Questions are asked only about the task cut, the phase boundaries and open items in the design that change the breakdown.
R22
Everything else asimov-spec decides itself and records as an assumption in the S101.
R23
asimov-spec finishes once, when the S101 and every S102 are written and validated, with the plan view and the findings that remain in chat; the waits before that are the go at the cut (R24) and, only when the finalised S101 differs from the one the author approved, a second approval (R28).
R24
Before the first S102 is written, asimov-spec shows the cut as the plan view with what the checks left, one line per finding, and waits for the author's go, which approves the plan (R15). Free text re-cuts and shows again; an S101 edited by hand is read as the graph, not re-cut; stop ends the run with the S101 on disk and no S102.
R25
Run against an S101 that has no S102s (after a stop, or after R11 stopped a run), asimov-spec shows the cut and waits for the go before it writes any S102.
R28
A change to the S101 after the go, by a re-cut, by the finalise step or by hand, invalidates the approval: asimov-spec shows the plan view with what changed and asks the author to approve again before ready. An S101 whose content is unchanged keeps its approval and is not asked twice.
R29
The checks run before the author sees anything: a Fail in the graph checks on a cut is fed back to the authoring skill as cut feedback, up to three times, without being shown; the author sees the plan view and only what remains, each finding as one line in words. A raw report row is never printed to the chat.
R30
Every authoring and validation call runs in a fresh subagent; the chat shows one line per write and one per check, and the method's output and the reports stay out of the conversation. The reports go to the sidecar. Every message of a run ends with what happens next or what the author must do.
R26
asimov-spec takes its target from the words of the message that names it: a D101 slug or path, a Jira link or ticket key, or pasted ticket text. With none, it says what it accepts and finishes. No flags, no argument order.
R27
For a ticket, asimov-spec fetches the ticket and its comments where the harness can, otherwise takes the pasted text, and keeps the text as a gitignored cache beside the plan. The S101 links the ticket and carries every design id with the source's wording, so the plan and the ticket link are all a reviewer needs; the cache is for the blind reader and is never a record.
Non-functional — 6

No product entity in templates or skills (hard rule 7).
NF2
Specs are Markdown with YAML frontmatter; the task graph is machine-readable in the S101 frontmatter.
NF3
Skills read the definitions and templates by path at run-time, relative to the plugin, in whichever harness runs them (hard rule 3).
NF4
The Spec workflow writes only under documentation/specs/<slug>/.
NF5
The two asimov-skills start only when a person names them; no harness selects them for the model.
NF6
One file per skill serves both Claude Code and Codex; nothing in the Spec workflow exists twice.
Out of scope — 6
OOS1
Dispatch and the ledger — the Build workflow; designed once a real S101 has been run.
OOS2
Verification, code against S102 acceptance criteria: asimov-build-verify and the role-s102-verifier skill — the Build phase.
OOS3

OOS4

OOS5

4
Business design
Principles, concepts, the Spec workflow and its authoring loop, the two artifacts, the rules, the plan lifecycle, and the decisions.

§4.1Principles

1
The spec is the product. What gets reviewed is the S101 and the S102s; code is the output.
2
Plan ≠ ledger. Run state never lives in a spec.
3
Cut vertically. One slice per buildable acceptance criterion; the checkpoint is the criterion met.
4
Skeleton before bodies. Phase 0 turns the interfaces into code before anyone builds against them.
5
Validate blind. Whoever wrote the S102 does not test it.
6
Shape checks by a fixed checklist. The model answers the same numbered checks every run; a script is the fallback if two runs disagree.
7
Ask only what cannot be discovered. The design and the repo answer first.
8
Method in the artifact skills, conversation in the asimov-skill. One file per skill; Claude Code and Codex read the same files.
9
Show the cut before the expansion. The cheapest place to correct a plan is before its task specs exist; the author sees the whole graph and says go.
10
One mechanism, two sources. A ticket is planned exactly like a D101; only where the ids come from differs. A one-task ticket gets a one-task plan, never a task spec without a plan.

§4.3The Spec workflow

One design goes in on the left, a D101 or a ticket; one S101 and N S102s come out on the right. The asimov-skills carry the conversation, the artifact skills carry the method, and the same four serve both.

Yours: the repository Asimov writes Asimov reads, then validates Fresh subagent, no conversation history
  • In: one design, named in the message: a D101 at Full design, or a Jira ticket that meets the design contract. Anything else is refused (R2, R26).
  • asimov-spec reads the design and the repo, asks at most 5 questions with defaults, cuts the plan and shows it, waits for the author's go, then uses the authoring skills to write and the validation skills to check, task by task and then the whole (R19–R25).
  • The author sees the cut before any task spec exists, after the checks have had their rounds (R29), corrects it in chat or in the file, and approves it with the go (R24, R15). A plan changed after the go is approved again (R28).
  • Out: one S101 and N S102s in documentation/specs/<slug>/: phase 0 skeleton per stack, then per slice one builder task per stack and one tester acceptance-test task (R1, R3–R7).
  • The re-check is asimov-spec named on a ready plan (R14): the same validation skills on every task spec and the plan, after a hand edit or a fresh checkout; a broken approval ends in the question to approve again (R28). It was a skill of its own, asimov-spec-validate, until 2026-10-07.
The authoring loop

The loop starts at the author's go. Each S102 is validated blind as it is written; the plan is validated once every task exists. A finding at either level sends the affected task back; a third failure, or a finding that points at the D101, stops the run.

Start / decision Delegated to a fresh subagent Finish
  1. 00
    Before the loop: the cut is on disk, checked and shown, and the author has said go, which is recorded as the approval (R24, R15, R29). Free text re-cuts and shows again; an S101 edited by hand is read as the graph; stop ends the run with no S102. The same gate opens a run that finds a cut without S102s (R25).
  2. 01
    A fresh writer subagent writes one S102 for one builder role from the S101 graph (R8, R30); the chat shows one line.
  3. 02
    A fresh read-only subagent validates it against the S102 checks, seeing only the S102, the S101, the design and the repo (R9). Clears → the S102 is marked validated, the agent's approval (R15). Fails → rewrite → 02 again (R10); a third failure, or a finding that names a design gap, stops the run with a report (R11).
  4. 03
    With every task written, a fresh subagent validates the plan against the S101 checks: coverage both ways, interface closure, disjoint parallel sets, no cycle, no contradicted constraint, size warnings (R12, R18, R30). A finding sends the affected tasks back to 01 (R13). Then the finalise: the S101 unchanged since the go → ready; changed → the author sees what changed and approves again, then ready (R28).

§4.6Plan lifecycle

One status axis on the S101. The Spec workflow moves it as far as ready; the Build workflow moves the rest.

Start state In progress Terminal
  • asimov-spec writes and re-writes draft; when the D101's intent changed it rewrites the plan from scratch, and git keeps the old one (R16, R17).
  • The cut is a draft S101 with no S102 beside it; no status value marks it, the folder does (R24, R25).
  • asimov-spec sets ready when the author's approval still matches the plan's fingerprint and every S102 is validated (R15); a change to the S101 after the go sends it back to draft until the author approves again (R28).
  • The Build workflow moves ready → in progress → done; out of scope here. done needs every checkpoint met and a reviewer's word on each criterion the coverage map marks verified by review; the ledger records who and when, the plan never does (S101 definition §6).

§5UI Design

The feature's only surface is the chat of one run; the mockup is styled after a terminal transcript, one column, monospace. Four moments in order; the gate between the second and the third is where the human sits in the loop, and the go there is the approval.

§5.1 The run as a transcript
  • Questions. asimov-spec says what it read, then asks at most five, one at a time, each with a default the author can take with one word (R19–R21).
  • The cut. The plan view, in the same shape every run (§5.2), with what the checks left as one line each (R29), followed by the gate (§5.3). Nothing but the S101 is on disk at this point.
  • The expansion. One line per task spec written and one per blind validation, from the subagents that did the work (R30); a failed check shows as the rewrite it caused. The author reads along but is not asked anything.
  • The finish. The plan view again, the findings that remain as one line each, the assumptions to read, and the status: ready, approved by you on the date, or what changed since the go in up to five lines and the question to approve again (R15, R23, R28).
  • Scenario tabs in the mockup switch between the full run, a ticket run and the states of §5.4 (the stop after a failed task is described, not shown); Replay restarts the one shown.

S101 as the author left it; no S102.
stop
Ends the run and says how to resume. The next run opens at the same gate (R25).
S101 alone; no S102.
  • The go is the author's approval of the plan (R15); the task specs are approved by the agents that check them. One person, one plan, read once; a plan changed after the go is read and approved again (R28).
  • The go is written into the S101 header as the approval: who, date, fingerprint (R15). It does not count against the five questions (R20).
§5.4 States
  • Refused. The D101 is not at Full design, or the ticket misses a required member of the design contract: one line naming what is missing and where to add it; nothing written (R2).
  • Ticket run. The message names a Jira link or key: the ticket and its comments are fetched where the harness can, or the pasted text is taken; the chat shows the approval found and the ids assigned to the ticket's items, then the run continues as for a D101 (R27).
  • Resumed at the cut. An S101 without S102s is found: the id comparison is reported, no questions are asked, the cut is shown and the gate waits (R25).
  • Stopped by a failed task. A third failure or a design gap: the run ends with the task named and the finding quoted; the next run resumes at the gate with the cleared S102s skipped (R11, R16).
  • Approve again. The finalise changed the S101: the plan view, what changed in up to five lines, and the question; approve → the new approval is written and the plan is ready (R28).
  • Re-check. asimov-spec on a ready plan: intact, one line and the offer of a blind re-check; edited by hand, every task spec checked again, failing ones rewritten, then the plan view, what changed and the question to approve again (R14, R28).

One subsection per box in §4.3: the pieces, the two asimov-skills, the four artifact skills, the specs folder.

§6.1The pieces

One contract, two definitions, two templates, six skills (four of method, two asimov-skills), a fresh subagent per skill call, one folder. The definitions set the bars, the templates the shape, the artifact skills the method, the asimov-skills the conversation; each is specified in §6.2–§6.8. §6.2asimov-spec

One run, lane by lane, in two figures split at the author's go. The asimov-skill owns when: the conversation, the gate and every decision. The skills own what and how, and every one of them runs in a fresh subagent: the authoring skills in a writer, the validation skills in a validator; the chat shows one line per call (R30). The gate is a when, so no skill knows it exists.

The cut · up to the author's go
Entry, the asimov-skill's steps and the author's decision Delegated to a fresh subagent · continues in the next figure Stop
The expansion · after the go
Entry, the asimov-skill's decisions and the author's approval Delegated to a fresh subagent Finish
  1. 01
    Asimov-skill. Read the words of the message: a D101 slug or path, a Jira link or key, pasted ticket text, or nothing, in which case say what is accepted and finish (R26). For a D101: read it, the conventions and the specs folder; refuse unless Full design (R2). For a ticket: fetch it with its comments where the harness can, else take the pasted text (which must carry the link, the contract's reference); check every required member of the design contract and refuse naming the missing one; otherwise write the cache (§6.8) with ids on its items (R, AC, OOS, NF), the ids an existing plan already holds kept, and show them (R27). Existing S101 at ready or later: refused with the reason; the author sets it back to draft first (R15). Existing S101 at draft: the design's R, NF and AC ids against the coverage map; an id missing or added → rewrite (R17); ids equal → one question, default refined → update in place (R16): every existing S102 goes through the blind call (§6.6) first, and the ones that clear are passed to step 02 as untouchable. Then at most five questions with defaults, only about the cut, the phases and open D101 items (R19–R21); an unanswered decision takes its default and becomes an assumption (R22).
  2. 02
    artifact-s101-authoring (§6.4), in a fresh writer subagent, cuts the plan from the design, the repo, the answers and any cut feedback; writes the S101 draft to disk before any S102, so the subagents and a resumed run read it from the folder; returns one line. On an update it receives the list of S102s that already clear and leaves them byte-identical (R16).
  3. 03
    Asimov-skill. Run artifact-s101-validation in graph-only mode in a fresh subagent (§6.7); a Fail goes back to 02 as cut feedback, up to three times, unseen (R29); a third Fail, or a row that names a gap in the design, stops the run (R11). Then print the plan view with what remains, one line each (§5.2), and ask: go, correct, or stop (R24). A run that found a cut without S102s enters here (R25).
  4. 04
    Author. go → the approval is written into the S101 header, who, date and the fingerprint of the plan's content (R15) → 05. Free text → 02 with the text as cut feedback. read again after a hand edit → 03 with the S101 on disk as the graph; a Fail is re-cut around the edit. stop → 08 with the S101 alone on disk. The go is not counted among the five questions (R20).
  5. 05
    artifact-s102-authoring (§6.5), in a fresh writer subagent, writes one S102 for one role from one graph entry (R8, R30); one line in the chat.
  6. 06
    artifact-s102-validation (§6.6) runs in a fresh subagent and returns a report (R9). The asimov-skill decides: clear → validated with the date in the S102 header, the agent's approval (R15), next task; fail → 05 (R10); third failure or design gap → stop (R11).
  7. 07
    artifact-s101-validation (§6.7) runs in a fresh subagent in full mode once every task exists (R12, R18, R30); a finding sends the affected tasks to 05 (R13), a row only the plan can fix goes to 02 as cut feedback.
  8. 08
    Asimov-skill. Finalise the S101 through artifact-s101-authoring in a writer subagent (contracts, coverage, assumptions), write the sidecar (§6.8), then compare the S101 with the approval's fingerprint: equal → status: ready; changed → print the plan view and what changed in up to five lines, ask the author to approve again, write the new approval, then ready (R28). Print the view and the findings that remain, one line each, and finish (R23). After a stop at the gate: print how to resume and finish with no sidecar. Only one line per call stays in context; the reports live in the sidecar (R30).

§6.3The re-check

A path of asimov-spec, mode recheck: the same two validations on a plan that is already ready, after a hand edit or a fresh checkout. Folded in from asimov-spec-validate on 2026-10-07, so each stage has one entry point.

  • Entered when the named plan is ready: with the approval intact, after one question (re-check or stop); with the fingerprint broken, at once, since the approval no longer holds (R28).
  • Runs artifact-s102-validation on every S102 in the folder through the same blind call (§6.6), rewrites a task spec that fails as an update run would, then artifact-s101-validation (§6.7), each in a fresh subagent (R30); a missing design cache is rebuilt first.
  • Ends as every run does (§6.2 step 08): the plan view, what changed, the findings as one line each, the reports in S101-<slug>.review.md (§6.8); an unchanged plan whose task specs clear returns to ready, a changed one asks for the approval again.
  • Asks only the one question on an intact plan; never the five.
  • Without a subagent, in a session that cannot spawn one, the S102 validation runs inline and the report carries blind: false (Q3).

§9Open questions

Q13Resolved 2026-10-06
Who approves what, and where is it recorded? v0.16 had a reviewer who is not the author approve the plan after the author's self-preview, and the author set ready by hand; the first real run did neither, and the author read every report row in the chat.
Decision: people approve plans, agents approve task specs (R15). The go at the cut is the approval, written with a fingerprint of the plan; a plan changed afterwards is approved again (R28). The checks run before the cut is shown and every skill call runs in a subagent, so the author reads the plan view and one line per finding, never a report (R29, R30). Reopens if authors approve a cut they did not read, or if the fingerprint flips on edits that change nothing.
Q12Allan Borup
Can the fresh validation subagent reach Jira itself through the Atlassian connector in Claude Code? If so, a Claude Code run needs no design cache, and the cache would be for Codex alone.
Resolution path: the first ticket run in Claude Code; until then the cache is written in both harnesses, because a blind reader without the text cannot check traceability.
Q8Allan Borup
When does the model-run shape checklist become a script?
Resolution path: the first time AC15 fails, i.e. two runs on an unchanged plan disagree; then a Node script under resources/, Node because Claude Code itself runs on it.
Q9Allan Borup
Which role writes the acceptance test of an Angular-only slice? Calvin is the .NET tester (R7).
Resolution path: the first Angular slice; either an Angular tester role skill, or Daneel writes it test-first under R7 and the D101-subagents roster says so.
Q11Resolved 2026-10-02
One spelling of the plugin paths that both harnesses resolve. ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT} is Claude Code's; Codex substitutes nothing and resolves a relative path from the working directory.
Decision: the derived-root method of D101-codex-support.html §6.2, in every skill that reads a plugin file: the Claude Code variable, and in Codex the root cut from the skill's own path, verified by reading the definition, with a search of the plugin cache as the fallback. A relative path was the rejected alternative. Reopens if the first Codex run (AC20) does not find the definition.
Resolved during authoring
Q10Resolved 2026-10-01
Where is the human between the five questions and the end of the run? A reader of v0.9 could not see the plan before every task spec was written.
Decision: a soft gate inside the run (R24, R25): the cut is shown and the author says go, corrects, or stops; §5 shows it, §4.7 records the alternatives. Reopens if authors routinely skip the cut with an immediate go.
Q1Resolved 2026-09-29
The S101 definition has no home for the assumptions R22 requires.
Decision: a §4.9 Assumptions section in the S101 definition and an Assumptions section last in the body (§6.4, §6.8).
Q2Resolved 2026-09-29
The size thresholds' numbers (3 and 7 files, ~100 lines, ~60 minutes) and their place in the S101 definition.
Decision: a thresholds block in the S101 definition, read by both validation skills (§6.6, §6.7); above three owned non-test files warns (AC9), the other numbers are set with the template.
Q3Resolved 2026-09-29
Blind validation in a session that cannot spawn a subagent.
Decision: the skill runs inline and the report carries blind: false (§6.3, §6.6); re-validate from a harness with subagents before review.
Q4Resolved 2026-09-29
Where a run's findings and assumptions land: chat only, or a sidecar like asimov-design-review's .review.md.
Decision: assumptions in the S101 body, findings in a gitignored S101-<slug>.review.md (§6.8).
Q5Resolved 2026-09-29
Who writes the tests of an S102?
Decision: the builder writes unit tests inline; a tester role writes the slice's acceptance test as its own S102, which is the phase checkpoint (§4.7). Reopens if a slice's acceptance test cannot be written before its code.
Q6Resolved 2026-09-29
Where does the code skeleton belong?
Decision: phase 0 Foundation, one S102 per stack (§4.7). Reopens if the skeleton tasks turn out to need judgement rather than transcription.
Q7Resolved 2026-09-29
Interview the developer, or plan from the D101?
Decision: plan from the D101 and the repo, ask at most 5 front-loaded questions with defaults, stop once (§4.7). Reopens if runs regularly exhaust the five.
