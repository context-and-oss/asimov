D101 · Feature design · project management

Risk log

The first artifact of the project-management family, the documentation that runs an engagement: one register per product repo, kept in the project's language, where every state beyond Active is a decision with a name on it and every change leaves a dated line.

Owner
Simon Vilandt
Business approver
Allan Borup
Family
project management
Pieces
1 asimov-skill · 2 skills · 1 script
Last updated
2026-10-21
Lands in
documentation/project/
Scope: the risk log, first member of the project-management family: the register, the entry point, the two skills and the script that maintain and show it, the Project section on a product's documentation site, and the family's pages on the Asimov website. It also sets what every member of the family shares (R1–R3); each later member (closure report, project setup, status report and their kin) gets its own D101 in the D101-project-management-* series. §2–§5 are the business agreement, §6 the technical design, §7 the build notes until the Spec stage plans it.

§2Why

The delivery model asks for a risk register reviewed every week, and Asimov can produce every document a solution needs but none of the documents that run the engagement.

  • Nothing covers the engagement. Every artifact the toolkit ships describes the solution; what could derail the work, who owns it and what changed since last week has no home.
  • Seen on our own projects: a register never reopened. A register was opened at kick-off and not opened again.
  • Seen on our own projects: a risk raised too late. A risk was raised only after it had happened, when it could no longer be mitigated, only reported.

The delivery model's risk discipline names the failures a register must prevent; their cost on our projects has not been measured.

  • The register that is written once. Opened at kick-off, never reopened, discovered at closure, and worse than none.
  • Mitigations with no owner. A plan nobody is answerable for reads as management while nothing is being done.
  • Accepting by default. A risk left standing with no decision looks the same as one the team chose to live with.
  • A plan read as a result. A register that shows a risk as mitigated because a plan was written gives false comfort.
Audience. The project lead who keeps the register, the risk owners, the customer and steering readers who read it on the product's documentation site under Project, and the maintainers who add the next project artifacts to this family.
Success. One engagement's register has been maintained five times, by a write or a confirmed review, by someone other than its author; that is also the evidence that moves the risk log from assess to trial.

4
Business design
The principles the family holds to, the vocabulary of the risk log, what a person does with it, the rules with their counter-examples, and the decisions with the alternatives they ruled out.

§4.1Principles

1
Engagement state, not solution documentation. A project artifact is maintained and re-read for the life of the engagement, not approved once and then read.
2
The page is the register. Data, view and history live on one page in the repo; there is no second store to fall out of step with it.
3
Nothing derived is read back. The score is shown on the page, but it is always recomputed from impact and probability, never taken from what the page shows.
4
Every decision is explicit and signed. Anything beyond Active is set by a person with a reason, and "nobody decided" is never a state.
5
The tool keeps the register; people hold the conversation. Asimov maintains the entries and never writes the message or chooses what to escalate.
6
One fixed view, learned once. The register always reads the same way, so the weekly review reads it top-down without relearning it.

§4.2Concepts

The vocabulary of the family and of its first member, in the words a project lead uses.

Concept
What it means
Project artifact
A document about the engagement rather than the solution, kept under documentation/project/ in the product repo.
Risk log (register)
The one page per product repo that holds every risk of the engagement, at documentation/project/risk-log.html.
Opt-out
A recorded decision, in the register's place, that no register is kept here, with its reason and the person who decided.
Risk
An uncertain event that could affect scope, schedule, resources, cost or quality, stated as an event rather than a worry.
Identifier
RISK-NNN, the stable handle a risk is updated by, numbered in one continuous series.
Owner
The named person who answers for the risk; a role is not an owner.
Impact
If it happens, what it costs, judged from 1 to 5 against the anchors on the page.
Probability
How likely it is to happen, judged from 1 to 5 against the anchors on the page.
Score
Impact times probability, from 1 to 25; it ranks risks against each other and does not say what to do.
Mitigation
The plan to avoid the risk or reduce its impact; written, it is a plan, not a result.
State
One of Active, Mitigated, Accepted, Occurred or Resolved; every state but Active is a person's decision.
Occurred
The risk happened: it is no longer uncertain, it is a problem, and it is not the same as a risk that was dealt with.
Open risk
A risk in any state but Resolved.
History
The dated, signed lines that record every change to one risk, or every update to the register, and only ever grow.
Dates
A risk has created and changed; the register has last updated.
Review
A person has read the register and confirmed it, which updates the register without changing any risk.
Reviewed by
The person who confirmed the review, which does not mean they reviewed alone; who else took part may be named with it.
Project language
The language the register's content is kept in, which may differ from the language a person writes in.

§4.3What a person does

What every member of the family has in common, and the seven things a person does with the risk log.

The family

Every member lands under documentation/project/, is listed under Project on the product's documentation site, is maintained through one named entry point in both tools, and is designed in its own D101 in the D101-project-management-* series.

  • The risk log is the first member; the closure report and project setup are the next two this document already names (OOS1, OOS2).
  • A member that is engagement state, like the register, hands nothing to a next stage and so has no stage contract (OOS9).
What a person can do with the risk log
  • Keep it by asking. Register, change, decide and hand over risks by asking for them by name (asimov-risk), in Claude Code or Codex.
  • Read it as a page. The register is a page on the product's documentation site, under Project, that the team and the customer read.
  • See it in the chat. The same view every time, so the weekly review reads it top-down.
  • Find the family on the Asimov website. Its pages say the family exists and what the risk log does.
F1 · Register a risk

"Something could derail us." — the person names a new risk; the four required fields are asked for, the three optional ones offered.

  • Outcome: a new risk, Active, with the next identifier, the history line Created, created and changed set to today, and the register updated today.
  • When no register exists yet, this first risk creates it, and the project language is asked for once (RL16).
  • When an opt-out stands, the register replaces it and its first history line names the opt-out it replaced (R23).
  • Told in another language than the project's, the risk is shown as it will be stored, in the project's language, and written only once the person confirms or corrects it (RL16).
  • A required field still missing after the questions: nothing is written, and the person is told which field is missing.
F2 · Change a risk

"Its impact went up." — the person names an identifier, sees the current values and says what changes.

  • Outcome: the fields changed, one history line naming each field with its old and new value, and the risk's changed date moved to today.
  • Any field can be changed in any state, a Resolved risk included, and the state stays as it is (R29).
  • An identifier that does not exist: nothing is written, and the register is shown so the person can find the right one.
  • Emptying the mitigation of a Mitigated risk: refused; the person first sets the risk back to Active with a reason (RL6).
F3 · Decide a risk's state

"We have dealt with it." or "It happened." — the person sets Mitigated, Accepted, Occurred, Resolved or back to Active, with a reason.

  • Outcome: the new state, a history line with the reason and the person, and the risk's changed date moved to today.
  • A state whose requirements are not met (no mitigation written, no decider named, no reason): the state is not set, and the person is told what is missing.
F4 · Review the register

"Where do we stand?" — the default when the entry point is called with nothing else; the view is shown with every open risk in full (R30), then the person is asked whether to mark the register as reviewed today.

  • Yes: one optional question follows, "Who took part?", which the person may skip; the register's last-updated date moves to today and its history gains a Reviewed line with the person and any participants; no risk changes.
  • No: nothing is written.
  • No register and no opt-out: the person is told so and offered F1 or F5.
F5 · Opt out

"We don't keep a risk register here." — the person gives the reason, the place when it is handled elsewhere, and who decided.

  • Outcome: the register's place holds the opt-out, with its reason, decider and date, and the site lists it as an ordinary card titled No risk register kept here, described by its reason.
  • An opt-out with a reason missing, a decider missing, or "handled elsewhere" without the place: nothing is written.
F6 · Bring a register back into working order

"Someone edited it by hand." — any flow that finds the register unreadable stops before writing and offers a repair instead.

  • Outcome: a repair proposal listing each change as before and after; once confirmed, the register is readable again, nothing it held is lost, and its history gains a Repaired line.
  • The repair declined: nothing is written, and the register stays unreadable until it is repaired, by the tool or by hand.
F7 · Hand risks to a new owner

"Jonas rolls off at the end of the month." — the person names the leaving owner and the named person who takes over.

  • Outcome: every open risk of that owner has the new owner, each with a history line naming the handover, and each risk's changed date moved to today.
  • Resolved risks keep the owner they had when they were resolved.
  • A new owner given as a role, or an owner with no open risks: nothing is written, and the person is told why.

§4.4Rules

What governs every entry and every change, each with the adjacent case it does not cover.

RL1 · A risk needs its four required fields

A risk is written only with a name, an owner who is a named person, an impact and a probability.

  • "Sponsor changes mid-engagement", owner Allan Borup, impact 4, probability 3: written.
  • The same risk with no owner, or with "the project lead" as owner: not written; the person is asked for a name.
RL2 · The score is computed, never given

The score shown is always impact times probability.

  • Impact 4, probability 3: the score shown is 12.
  • "Score it 15": declined; the person is asked for impact and probability instead.
RL3 · Identifiers continue the series

A new risk takes the highest identifier in the register plus one, counting resolved risks.

  • RISK-001 to RISK-006, with RISK-004 resolved: the new risk is RISK-007.
  • RISK-007 resolved and the highest: the next is RISK-008, never RISK-007 again.
RL4 · Duplicates are renumbered by the tool

When two risks share an identifier, the next write first gives the later-created one the highest identifier plus one, with its history and a Renumbered from line, and only then makes its own change; created the same day, the one further down the page is renumbered.

  • Two branches each added RISK-007 and both were merged, RISK-008 exists: the later one becomes RISK-009.
  • The same register, and the next write adds a risk: the duplicate becomes RISK-009 first, and the new risk RISK-010.
  • No identifier is shared: nothing is renumbered, and a gap in the series is never closed.
RL5 · Any state from any state, each with a reason

Every state can be set from every other, each target state brings its own requirements, and every change of state carries a reason.

  • Mitigated to Accepted, because what is left after the mitigation is lived with: set, with the reason.
  • Active to Active is no change of state: nothing is written.
RL6 · Mitigated needs a written mitigation

A risk can be set Mitigated only while its mitigation field holds a plan.

  • Mitigation written, person sets Mitigated: set.
  • Mitigation empty: the person is asked for it, and without one the risk stays where it was.
  • The mitigation of a Mitigated risk is emptied: refused; the risk is first set back to Active with a reason.
RL7 · Accepted needs a reason and a decider

A risk can be set Accepted only with a reason and the named person who decided to live with it.

  • "We live with it: the customer carries the cost. Allan Borup": set, and the history line names both.
  • "We live with it" with no name: not set.
RL8 · A risk's dates

A risk's created date is set once; its changed date moves on anything that touches that risk, its fields, its state or its identifier.

  • A new owner is set: changed moves to today, created stays.
  • A confirmed review: no risk's changed date moves.
RL9 · The register's date

The register's last-updated date moves on every write and on every confirmed review, and each move adds a line, with the person, to the register's history.

  • "Yes, mark it reviewed", with Erik Lund and Jonas Berg taking part: last updated is today, and the register's history gains Reviewed by Maja Holm with Erik Lund, Jonas Berg.
  • The register shown and the question answered no: nothing moves.
RL10 · History only grows

Every change adds a dated line naming the person, and no action removes or rewrites a line.

  • Resolving a risk adds a line; the lines before it stand.
  • A typo in a risk's name is corrected: a new line records the correction, and the earlier lines still show the old name; the history is never tidied, even when a line is wrong.
RL11 · An unreadable register is repaired, never overwritten

A register the tool cannot read in full is not written to; the tool proposes a repair, returns each change as before and after, and writes it only when the person confirms, losing nothing the register held.

  • A risk lost its impact in a hand edit: the proposal shows RISK-005 impact: (missing) → asked and waits for the value and the go.
  • A register that reads in full: no repair is proposed.
RL12 · How the register is ordered

Risks are grouped by state in the order Active, Mitigated, Accepted, Occurred, Resolved, and within a state by score, highest first, then by identifier.

  • Two Active risks at 12: RISK-003 before RISK-009.
  • An Active risk at 20 comes before one at 12, even when the one at 12 changed more recently: the order never follows dates.
RL13 · An area is the person's word

An area is free text and the tool never proposes one; only when the area given is nearly the same as one already used does it ask once, and the person's answer stands.

  • "schedule" given, "Schedule" already used: the tool asks "Did you mean Schedule?" and keeps what the person answers.
  • No area given, or "Supplier" when no area resembles it: nothing is asked, and the area is kept as written or left empty.
RL14 · What the closure report must honour (family rule)

When the engagement closes, the register is reviewed first and the risks no longer relevant are resolved; every risk not Resolved then carries into the closure report.

  • An Accepted or Occurred risk at closure: carried into the report, since it transfers with the solution.
  • A Resolved risk: not carried.
RL15 · An opt-out needs its reason and decider

An opt-out is written only with one of the two reasons, the person who decided and, for handled elsewhere, the place.

  • "Handled in the customer's tracker, project X; Allan Borup": written.
  • "Handled elsewhere" with no place: not written.
RL16 · Content is kept in the project's language

Content is kept in the project's language; when a person writes in another language, the tool shows the translation it will store and writes it only once the person has confirmed it or corrected it in the project's language.

  • An English project, "kunden når ikke at frigive testere før uge 48": shown as "The customer cannot release testers before week 48. Correct?" and written only after the answer.
  • Written in the project's language: no translation step, and the words are stored as written.
  • It covers the text the person writes (name, description, mitigation, reasons); history lines the tool phrases itself are written straight in the project's language, and names of people and systems are never translated.
  • The language is asked for when the register or an opt-out is first written.
RL17 · Occurred needs what happened and where it is handled

A risk can be set Occurred only with a reason saying what happened and where it is handled now.

  • The customer's test environment arrives three weeks late: set Occurred, "arrived 2026-11-17, three weeks late; re-planned in the weekly plan".
  • A risk that did not happen because the mitigation worked: Resolved, not Occurred.

§5UI Design

Four surfaces change: the register page, what the entry point prints, the card on the product's site and the family's pages on the Asimov website. Styled after the existing documentation site chrome (the register page), the plan view of the Spec stage (the chat) and the Asimov website (its pages).

S1 · The register page
  • From the top: title, last-updated date with its ⓘ, the count per state; the scoring anchors folded; the summary table; the risks grouped by state.
  • Each summary row links to its risk; each risk shows its fields, created and changed dates, and its history with who made each change.
  • The ⓘ beside the last-updated date unfolds the register's own history: every update and every confirmed review, with date and person.
  • Occurred risks have their own group, after Accepted; Resolved risks sit last and folded.
  • Headings and labels follow the template; the content is in the project's language (RL16).
  • Switch the state at the top of the mockup: a register with risks, a register where every risk is resolved, and an opt-out.
S2 · What the entry point prints
  • One fixed text layout, 80 columns: the header line, then the risks by state, each open risk in full (identifier, score and name; owner, area, impact, probability and changed date; description; mitigation), each risk closed by a horizontal rule so the risks are easy to tell apart, then the closing question.
  • An empty description or mitigation shows as none written, so a risk without a plan stands out in the review; Resolved risks show only as a count.
  • The chat's own words follow the conversation's language; a risk's content is shown as it is kept.
  • Scenarios: a review confirmed with its participants, a risk registered in Danish and confirmed in English, a state decided, risks handed to a new owner, no register yet, an opt-out, and an unreadable register with its repair shown as before and after.
  • The view never prints a verdict; anything the entry point says about the register comes after the view and is its own line.
S3 · The card on the product's documentation site
  • A Project section joins the site's catalogue; the register is one card in it, in the same form as every other document card: title, one-line description, HTML badge.
  • An opt-out is an ordinary card too: the site reads its title and description as it does for every page, and the opt-out page is titled No risk register kept here with its reason as description; the site does not know it is an opt-out.
  • The card shows no figures from the register (OOS3, Q1).

6
Technical design
Where the risk log lives, how one write flows from the conversation through a script to the page, the files, the page's data shape, the script's interface, the few settings, the failures and their recovery, and the gotchas.

§6.1Platform

Everything ships in the plugin and runs in Claude Code and Codex alike: one asimov-skill (asimov-risk), two artifact skills (artifact-risk-log-authoring, artifact-risk-log-view), one script (scripts/risk-log.ps1, Windows PowerShell 5.1 and PowerShell 7), and the artifact's definition and template under artifacts/documentation/risk-log/. In a product repo it writes one file, documentation/project/risk-log.html, and while a write is in flight its sibling risk-log.html.new. The script reads, renders and checks the page and compares areas; the skills hold the conversation and decide what changes. There is no service, no store beside the page and no network call.

⚠ Lockstep gates. The Project bucket changes five files that must move together (hard rule 9), the documentation/project/*.new pattern joins the .gitignore block written by both init entry points and appended by asimov-risk, and the artifact's assess level is mirrored in D100 §4.5 in the same commit (hard rule 10).

§6.2Data flow

One write, registering a risk: the conversation decides the change, the script turns it into a checked page, and the page is replaced only when the check passes.

Person, the entry Processing: the skills, the script The register page
1
Start. asimov-risk prints the maturity line, adds the four cache patterns and documentation/project/*.new to .gitignore when missing, and runs -Finish if a .new is left from an earlier run.
2
Read. -Read parses the page into a data model and a list of problems; any problem, conflict markers included, turns the run into a repair (-Repair, RL11) before anything else.
3
Decide. artifact-risk-log-authoring turns what the person said into one change file: the fields, the state change with its reason and decider, and every free text already in the project's language.
4
Confirm. asimov-risk shows the change as it will be stored, translation included; a correction replaces the proposed text as written.
5
Apply. -Apply renumbers duplicates first, applies the change, appends the history lines with the person from git config user.name, renders the page from the template to risk-log.html.new, checks it against the current page, and renames it into place.
6
Refuse. A failed check deletes the .new, leaves the page as it was, and returns the rule that refused it.
7
Review. -View prints the fixed view; a confirmed review is an -Apply whose change adds one register-history line and touches no risk.

Areas nearly the same (RL13)

Two areas are nearly the same when they are equal after ignoring case, spaces, hyphens and punctuation, or, for words of four letters or more, when they differ by one letter.

  • schedule, Schedule and sched-ule are nearly the same; so are Schedul and Schedule.
  • Cost and Costs are nearly the same; QA and QC are not, being shorter than four letters.
The change file -Apply takes

  • fields: name · owner · impact · probability · area · description · mitigation
  • state: { to, reason, decider }
  • with: [participants] (review) · to: new owner (handover) · opt-out: { reason, where, decider }
  • language: the project language, on the first write only

§6.5APIs & contracts

One script, risk-log.ps1 -Path documentation/project/risk-log.html plus one action: -Read (data model, the areas in use and problems as JSON; with -Area <text> also the areas in use that are nearly the same), -View (the fixed 80-column text), -Apply -Change <file> (write, check, rename), -Repair (a proposal as JSON, from problems or conflict markers) and -Finish (an interrupted write). Exit codes: 0 done, 1 refused by a rule (the rule on stdout), 2 an input could not be read, 3 no person (git config user.name unset and no -By). -PluginRoot defaults to the parent of the script's folder. No other interface changes.

§6.8Known constraints & gotchas

Encoding. Windows PowerShell 5.1 reads a BOM-less script as ANSI, so the script is pure ASCII like the validators, and it writes the page as UTF-8 explicitly, since names and content are rarely ASCII.
The rename is atomic only on one volume. The .new sits beside the page, never in a temp folder, so the replace stays one rename.
Hand edits to rendered text are overwritten. A changed score, summary row or count is rendered anew on the next write (P3, AC10); a hand edit to a field or a history line is a repair.
Line endings. The check compares the parsed data, not bytes, so a checkout that turns LF into CRLF never fails a write; only a declined review is promised byte-identical, and it writes nothing.

Q5Resolved 2026-10-19
Does a written mitigation make a risk Mitigated, as the brief proposed?
Decision: no; Mitigated is set by a person, only while a mitigation is written (R10, RL6). Reopened only if the register's readers find the extra step is skipped in practice.

Can the register express a deliberate acceptance?
Decision: yes, as the state Accepted with a reason and a named decider (R11, RL7).
