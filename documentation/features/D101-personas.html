Personas

The reader-persona review layer: a curated set of standard readers shipped with the toolkit so a repo has reviewers on day one, plus custom readers a team authors for its own audience — all named persona-*, listed together, and invoked by name.

Design
Scope: Design-stage tooling — a shipped standard set of reader personas plus custom authoring, listed together. §2–§4 are the business agreement; §6 is the technical design.

§2Why this exists

Reading a document as one of its intended readers — to find where it talks to its author instead of its reader — is valuable but not yet easy to reach for. A team hits it from both ends: there are no ready readers when a repo starts, and hand-authoring one's own is a toll that keeps the layer on the shelf.

∅
No readers on day one
Without a shipped set, every repo starts from zero — and each team's home-grown readers vary, so the same document gets a different review depending on who wrote the persona.
✍
Form knowledge is a toll
Authoring a persona by hand means knowing the template, the five fields, the fail/reward catalogue, and where the skill file lives. That toll is what keeps the layer from being picked up.
≠
Hand-authored readers drift
Written freehand, a persona quietly breaks its own job: one that reads as a rubber stamp, or one that "knows the codebase", fails to catch the very things — leaked jargon, condescension — it exists to catch.
⧉
The catalogue rots on copy
Each skill must carry its own copy of the generic fail/reward catalogue so it runs standalone. Copied by hand, that copy drifts from the standard the moment the standard moves.
⚙
Feasibility checked too late
The business design gets agreed, then an engineer finds the approach can't hold as drawn — no one read the design as a technical reviewer while changing it was still cheap.
Audience. The author working in a product repo — the same developer who runs the design commands, not an expert in the persona-review form. They know the reader they have in mind; they should not have to know the toolkit's machinery to reach for one or capture their own.

R12

4
Business design
Six principles, the five interview fields, the rules that govern them, the standard persona set, and the decisions behind the design.

§4.1Principles

1
Capture the reader, not the mechanism. A persona describes a person; the toolkit owns the form. What goes in is a reader, never a checklist.
2
The toolkit ships the form and the generic readers; the product ships the specific ones. Standard personas are archetypes with no product entity, so they travel with the toolkit; custom readers belong to the product (hard rule 7).
3
One persona, one skill; persona- is the namespace. Distinct readers are distinct skills, all named persona-<slug> — the prefix groups the set and marks a skill as a persona review (skill discovery is flat, so a folder cannot).
4
Generated skills are self-contained. The generic catalogue is inlined so a skill runs with no path back to the toolkit — it lives in the product repo and must not depend on it.
5
Flag, don't guess. A custom persona that cannot do its job is warned about and explained, not written in silence.
6
Create, then hand off. /persona-new creates a custom reader; /persona-list shows the whole roster; running the review is the skill's job, reached by name or through /d101-review — no one command does two.

§4.2The five fields

Every persona — a shipped standard one (§4.4) or a custom one a team authors — is defined by exactly these five, and nothing else. They are not biography: each field is what lets the review catch a specific kind of reader-hostility, so each carries a quality guard /persona-new checks (§4.3, R6).

§4.2.101
Identity
"Who is this reader, in one line?"
A real role in the product's world — the thing the review speaks as. Specific enough that a reader of the review pictures a person.
  • "a frontline support agent who onboards new customers every day"
  • "a business user" is too generic to stall anywhere — the guard flags it.
§4.2.202
Knows cold
"What domain do they live in?"
The expertise that lets them spot condescension (a definition of something they already know) and a made-up specific where they know the real one.
  • "the plan tiers, billing, account limits, how customers organise their teams"
  • Vague expertise = a reader who rewards vagueness; name real things.
§4.2.303
Does not know
"What are they blind to — and glad to be?"
Explicitly the codebase, architecture, and internal vocabulary. This blindness is what lets the reader catch leaked jargon and code-altitude prose.
  • "the codebase, the enums, the endpoints, our documentation conventions"
  • A reader who has read the code silently accepts the jargon they exist to catch.
§4.2.404
Has (the time budget)
"How long before they get back to their job?"
Minutes, not an afternoon. The budget is what makes ordering matter: material placed after the point they stop reading is material they never see.
  • "five to ten minutes, and a queue to get back to"
  • No budget and the review cannot say where the reader stops.
§4.2.505
Is reviewing to
"What are they there to correct — and not there to do?"
The facts they are there to fix (about people and work), and, as sharply, what they are not there to do — approve engineering.
  • "correct who can do what in the field, not sign off the design"
  • A reader asked to approve engineering is no longer a business stand-in.

§4.4Standard personas

Three shipped readers, one per orthogonal stake — named after Greek gods so a real person represented by one is flattered, not caricatured, and the handles read as personalities. Each ships as a plugin skill persona-<god>, invoked "review as <name>"; custom readers a team authors sit alongside them under the same persona- names.

Poseidon

A frontline operator who runs the work every day and talks to the people it affects.
Knows cold
How the process actually runs, the exceptions and workarounds, what users really do and complain about.
Does not know
The codebase, the architecture, the internal vocabulary — and doesn't want to.
Has
A few minutes between real tasks, and a job to get back to.
Is reviewing to
Correct facts about people and work — not to approve engineering or the budget.

6
Technical design
Where the commands and the standard set live, the flow /persona-new runs, the shape of a skill and how the lister finds both kinds, configuration, and how it fails.

§6.1Platform

Both commands are markdown prompt files in the Asimov plugin — commands/persona-new.md and commands/persona-list.md — with YAML frontmatter (description, argument-hint, model, allowed-tools). /persona-new is granted Read, Write, Glob; /persona-list is granted Read, Glob and is read-only. The standard set ships as plugin skills at plugins/asimov-plugin/skills/persona-{poseidon,athena,hermes}/SKILL.md, available in any repo the moment Asimov is installed; custom personas are written to the product repo's .claude/skills/persona-<slug>/.

⚠ Escalation gate. /persona-new writes a file into the product repo, so every write sits behind the confirmed write-plan (R8) — the same gate /asimov-init uses. No schema or configuration change is involved.

§6.2Data flow

The run /persona-new performs, end to end. One actor, with the advisory check, the maturity notice and the overwrite guard drawn as the numbered detail below rather than as branches.

Start / gate Process step Read / write a file Terminator
1
Read the contract. Load the persona-review standard and the persona template from the plugin at run time — never from memory (R10).
1b
Maturity notice. Resolve the definition's maturity: block to at most one chat line, printed before the first question; nothing for adopt; never written into the skill (R14, D101-artifact-maturity.html).
2
Interview. Ask the author for the five fields (§4.2), inferring from the brief and asking only the gaps.
3
Check, advisory. Match the answers against the definition's §7 anti-patterns; on a match, warn and name it — the author may override (R6, R7).
4
Slug + write-plan. Derive persona-<slug> from the reader's name (overridable), then show a plan naming the path and NEW or OVERWRITE; an existing skill offers overwrite or a new slug (R4, R8, R9).
5
Render + write. On confirmation, substitute the five answers into the template, strip its leading authoring comment, and write to .claude/skills/persona-<slug>/SKILL.md, creating the folder if absent (R2, R5).
6
Report. Name the written path and point the author at invoking the review by name — review as <name>.

§6.8Known constraints & gotchas

01
Custom skills drift
A custom skill inlines its own catalogue, so a change to the standard does not reach it — it must be regenerated. (Shipped standard skills update with the plugin.)
02
Prefix + clash
Recognition rests on the persona- prefix; a custom slug that reuses a shipped god's name collides, and §8 Q3 tracks how the clash resolves.
03
Two baked paths, three files
.claude/skills/ (custom) and skills/persona-* (standard) are literals in persona-new.md, persona-list.md and d101-review.md; a layout fork must change all three files in lockstep.
